Why This Matters

Buying the wrong amount of paint is one of the most common — and costly — DIY mistakes. Underestimate by even 10% on a project and you risk running out mid-wall, which leads to a visible color difference when you open a new can from a different batch (even the same color can vary slightly between production runs).

On the flip side, over-buying wastes money. A gallon of premium interior paint runs $35–$70. On a whole-house repaint — say, 4 bedrooms, a living room, kitchen, and hallways — the difference between a careful calculation and a rough guess can easily be 3–5 extra gallons, costing $150+ unnecessarily.

Professionals use a coverage rate of 350–400 sq ft per gallon for smooth walls. Rough textures, bare drywall, or deep color changes often need 250–300 sq ft/gallon because porous or highly absorptive surfaces drink more paint. For best results, always apply a primer first when making dramatic color changes — this counts as a separate coat in your calculation.

How It's Calculated

The paint coverage formula is straightforward:

Wall Area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height Net Area = Wall Area − Door Area − Window Area Total Area = Net Area + Ceiling Area (if included) Gallons = (Total Area × Coats) ÷ Coverage Rate

For example: a 15 × 12 ft room with 9 ft ceilings, 2 doors (3×6.8 ft each) and 2 windows (3×4 ft each), painted with 2 coats at 350 sq ft/gallon:

Wall Area = 2 × (15+12) × 9 = 486 sq ft Exclusions = 2×(3×6.8) + 2×(3×4) = 40.8 + 24 = 64.8 sq ft Net Area = 486 − 64.8 = 421.2 sq ft Gallons = (421.2 × 2) ÷ 350 = 2.41 gallons → buy 3 cans

The calculator automatically rounds up to the nearest can size so you never run short.

Worked Example

Take a 12 × 12 ft bedroom with a 9 ft ceiling, 2 doors, and 1 window, painted with 2 coats at 350 sq ft/gallon:

Wall Area = 2 × (12+12) × 9 = 432 sq ft Exclusions = 2×(3×6.8) + 1×(3×4) = 40.8 + 12 = 52.8 sq ft Net Area = 432 − 52.8 = 379.2 sq ft Gallons = (379.2 × 2) ÷ 350 = 2.17 gallons → buy 3 one-gallon cans

If you also paint the ceiling (144 sq ft), total area becomes 523.2 sq ft, and gallons needed rise to about (523.2 × 2) ÷ 350 = 2.99 gallons — still 3 one-gallon cans, but with almost no buffer left over, so many painters round up to a fourth can for touch-ups.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Always measure, never guess. Even a 1-foot error in a room's dimensions can mean 20–30 sq ft difference — about half a coat's worth on a small wall.
  • Use the right coverage rate. Default is 350 sq ft/gallon but textured walls or bare drywall may only cover 250 sq ft/gallon. Check your paint can's label — it's always printed there.
  • Don't skip the primer count. If you're painting over a dark color with a light one, count the primer as a separate coat. A primer coat at 300 sq ft/gallon for a 500 sq ft room is nearly 2 gallons on its own.
  • Buy a little extra for touch-ups. The "1-gallon cans to buy" result in this calculator already rounds up, giving you a small buffer. Store the remainder sealed in a cool, dark place — it lasts 2–5 years for touch-ups.
  • Ceiling paint is different. If you're painting the ceiling, use a dedicated ceiling paint rated at 300–350 sq ft/gallon and calculate it separately — or use this tool's "Include Ceiling" option for a combined estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does one gallon of paint cover?

Most interior latex paints cover 350–400 square feet per gallon on a smooth, previously painted surface. Rough textures, bare drywall, or highly porous surfaces typically get only 250–300 sq ft/gallon. Always check your specific paint's label — the coverage rate is always listed there.

Do I need to calculate primer separately?

Yes. Primer is a separate product with its own coverage rate (usually 200–300 sq ft/gallon for high-hide primers). If you're painting over a drastically different color, bare drywall, or a repaired surface, calculate primer as an additional coat. This tool handles it by letting you adjust the number of coats and coverage rate.

Should I subtract the area of doors and windows?

Yes — for interior rooms. A standard door is about 3×6.8 = 20.4 sq ft and a typical window is 3×4 = 12 sq ft. In a small room these can account for 15–20% of a wall, so subtracting them gives a meaningfully more accurate estimate. Use the exclusions section in the Room Calculator for this.

How many gallons do I need for a 12×12 room?

A 12×12 room with 9 ft ceilings has 432 sq ft of total wall area (2×(12+12)×9). Subtract 2 doors and 1 window (~52.8 sq ft), leaving about 379.2 sq ft. At 2 coats and 350 sq ft/gallon, that's roughly 2.17 gallons — round up and buy 3 one-gallon cans. Add the ceiling (144 sq ft) and you'll need closer to 3 gallons for 2 coats total.

How much paint do I need per square foot?

For one coat on a smooth wall, you'll use roughly 1/350th of a gallon per square foot (at 350 sq ft/gallon coverage). For two coats, double that — about 1/175th of a gallon per square foot. This calculator does that math automatically based on the coverage rate you enter.

Do I need more paint for dark or bold colors?

Yes, often. Dark and saturated colors, especially reds and deep blues, may need an extra coat for full, even opacity, and some manufacturers rate these paints at a slightly lower coverage rate. If you're using a bold color, consider bumping your coats setting to 3 in this calculator for a safer estimate.
