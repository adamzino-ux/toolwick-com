How to Use This Blown-In Insulation Calculator

Enter the attic floor area, pick fiberglass, cellulose or mineral wool, and set the target R-value with the slider. If insulation is already in the attic, enter its R-value so the calculator counts only the layer you need to add. You get the bags to buy (rounded up, waste included), the depth to blow, and an estimated material cost.

The optional coverage field is for the figure printed on your bag's chart. Enter the sq ft per bag listed at your target R-value. Leave it blank to use our typical figure for the material.

Why This Matters

Attic insulation is usually the cheapest energy upgrade in a house. Heat rises, and a thin or uneven layer lets it escape. ENERGY STAR estimates that air sealing plus adding insulation saves about 15% on heating and cooling costs, and attics are where most homes fall short of the roughly R-30 to R-60 that DOE and ENERGY STAR recommend.

The bag count is the number everyone plans around. Home centers often lend a blowing machine free with a minimum purchase, typically 10 or more bags, so ordering too few means a second trip with the job half done. Ordering too many ties up cash, though unopened bags are usually returnable.

Here are two typical jobs. A 1,200 sq ft attic taken from bare to R-38 with cellulose needs about 60 bags (roughly $1,440 at $24 per bag). The same attic topped up from R-19 to R-38 needs only about 30 bags (about $720). Homeowners, landlords, flippers and contractors quoting jobs all need that number before they buy.

How It's Calculated

Bag charts show that coverage per bag falls in proportion to the R-value you are installing, because the weight of material per square foot rises with R. So each material has one constant, K, the sq ft × R that one bag delivers. A bag covers K ÷ R square feet.

Added R = max(0, Target R − Existing R)
Settled depth (in) = Added R ÷ R-per-inch
Bags = ceiling( Area × Added R ÷ K × (1 + Waste%) )
Cost = Bags × Price per bag

We use settled R-per-inch of 2.5 for fiberglass, 3.5 for cellulose and 3.1 for mineral wool. K is 1,000 for fiberglass, 850 for cellulose and 900 for mineral wool. These are typical 25–35 lb bag figures averaged across brands, not any one product. If you enter your own chart coverage, the calculator uses it as the figure at your target R-value and scales it for the R you are adding. Typical coverage in sq ft per bag:

Rounding is deliberately conservative. Bags always round up, and depths round up to the next tenth of an inch. The "blow to" depth also adds an allowance for settling: about 15% for cellulose and 5% for the other two. The R-values follow the DOE and ENERGY STAR rule of thumb. Your locally adopted energy code (the IECC edition or a state amendment) sets the legal minimum.

Worked Example

A 1,200 sq ft attic, cellulose, target R-38, existing R-0, 10% waste, $24 per bag. These are the tool's default values.

  • Added R: 38 − 0 = R-38.
  • Settled depth: 38 ÷ 3.5 = 10.86, shown as 10.9 in. Blow to about 12.5 in before settling (10.86 × 1.15).
  • Coverage: 850 ÷ 38 = 22.37 sq ft per bag.
  • Bags before waste: 1,200 × 38 ÷ 850 = 53.65.
  • With 10% waste: 53.65 × 1.10 = 59.01, rounded up to 60 bags.
  • Cost: 60 × $24 = $1,440.

Now suppose the attic already has R-19. Added R drops to 19, so 1,200 × 19 ÷ 850 = 26.82 bags. Add 10% for waste and round up for 30 bags, or $720.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Be honest about existing R-value. Compressed, dusty or damp insulation delivers less than its label. If in doubt, enter a lower number, which adds a little extra material.
  • Air seal first. Caulk or foam gaps around wires, pipes and top plates before blowing. Use fire-rated materials around flues and chimneys.
  • Keep ventilation open. Install baffles at the soffits so insulation never blocks airflow. Attic ventilation requirements (IRC R806 commonly calls for 1 sq ft of vent per 300 sq ft of attic floor) still apply.
  • Respect clearances. Keep insulation away from flues, chimneys and non-IC-rated recessed lights, using dams or covers as your code and the manufacturer require.
  • Use depth markers and buy extra. Staple a ruler to a rafter every few hundred square feet. Blow to the initial depth for cellulose, since it settles.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of blown-in insulation do I need per 1,000 sq ft?

At R-38, 1,000 sq ft takes about 38 fiberglass bags or 45 cellulose bags before waste. With a 10% waste factor that becomes about 42 and 50 bags. Higher or lower R-values scale up or down in proportion.

What R-value do I need for my attic?

DOE and ENERGY STAR recommend roughly R-30 to R-60 for most U.S. attics. Warm southern zones sit at the low end and cold northern zones at the high end. Your local energy code sets the legal minimum, so check it before you buy.

Is cellulose or fiberglass better for blown-in attic insulation?

Cellulose gives more R per inch (about 3.5 versus 2.5) and is made from recycled paper, but it settles and is heavier. Fiberglass is lighter and not moisture-absorbent, but it needs more depth for the same R-value. Price and availability usually decide it.

Can I add blown-in insulation over existing insulation?

Yes, if the existing layer is dry and free of mold, and there is no vapor barrier facing up. Enter its R-value and the calculator subtracts it from your target. Fix any roof leaks or moisture problems first.

Why add a waste factor?

Material gets lost to spills, hose purging, uneven blowing and obstacles like ducts and wiring. Ten percent suits an open, easy attic. Use 15% or more for cramped attics with many obstructions.

Can I rent a blowing machine and do it myself?

Many home centers lend one free with a minimum bag purchase. Two people can usually finish a 1,200 sq ft attic in a few hours, one feeding the machine and one in the attic. Wear a respirator, goggles and coveralls.
