Why This Matters

Buying too little drywall mid-project means emergency hardware store runs and potential dye-lot mismatches. Buying too much wastes money and creates disposal headaches. For a typical 12×14 ft bedroom with 8-foot ceilings — including the ceiling and one standard door opening — you're looking at about 20 sheets of 4×8 drywall once waste is factored in, roughly $360 in material at $18/sheet (see the full worked example below).

Professional drywall estimators generally add a 10–15% waste factor to account for cuts around windows, outlets, and irregular shapes. First-time DIYers often skip this step and end up short. This calculator bakes that buffer in so you get it right the first time. Whether you're drywalling a single bedroom, a basement, or an entire house addition, accurate estimates help you negotiate better prices on bulk material orders and prevent costly mid-job delays.

How It's Calculated

Total Wall Area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Ceiling Height
Ceiling Area = Length × Width (if included)
Net Area = Total Area − Openings
Sheets = CEIL( Net Area × (1 + Waste%) ÷ Sheet Size )

Screws are estimated at roughly 1 screw per square foot of purchased drywall (about 32 for a 4×8 sheet, 48 for a 4×12 sheet), and the count scales automatically with the sheet size you choose. Joint tape is estimated at 0.4 linear feet per square foot of net drywall area — about 12–13 ft per 4×8 sheet, in line with typical contractor rules of thumb. Joint compound is estimated at roughly 0.053 gallons per square foot of finished area, or about one 4.5-gallon bucket per 80–85 sq ft.

Worked Example

Say you're drywalling a 12 ft × 14 ft bedroom with an 8 ft ceiling. You're including the ceiling, there's one standard door (about 20 sq ft to subtract), you're using 4×8 sheets (32 sq ft each), a 10% waste factor, and drywall costs $18/sheet.

  1. Wall area: 2 × (12 + 14) × 8 = 416 sq ft
  2. Ceiling area: 12 × 14 = 168 sq ft
  3. Gross area: 416 + 168 = 584 sq ft
  4. Net area (minus door): 584 − 20 = 564 sq ft
  5. With 10% waste: 564 × 1.10 = 620.4 sq ft
  6. Sheets needed: 620.4 ÷ 32 = 19.4 → rounds up to 20 sheets
  7. Material cost: 20 × $18 = $360
  8. Screws: 20 sheets × 32 = 640 screws (≈4 boxes of 200)
  9. Tape: 564 × 0.4 = 226 linear ft (≈1 roll of 250 ft)
  10. Joint compound: 564 × 0.053 = 29.9 gallons (≈7 buckets of 4.5 gal)

Plug these exact numbers into the calculator above to see the same results, then adjust the sheet size, waste percentage, or openings to match your own room.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Don't skip the waste factor. Even pros use 10–12% for simple rectangular rooms and 15–20% for rooms with angled ceilings or lots of windows.
  • Measure openings accurately. A standard exterior door is about 21 sq ft, an interior door is ~18–20 sq ft, and a typical double-hung window is 12–15 sq ft.
  • Choose the right thickness. 1/2" is standard for most walls. Use 5/8" Type X for fire-rated assemblies or garage walls adjacent to living space.
  • Account for tall walls. For 9- or 10-foot ceilings, consider 4×10 or 4×12 sheets to minimize horizontal seams and reduce taping work.
  • Order all at once. Many suppliers offer per-unit discounts at 50, 100, or 200+ sheet thresholds. It often costs less to round up to the next tier than to place a second order later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many screws do I need per sheet of drywall?
A common rule of thumb is roughly 1 screw per square foot of board — about 32 screws for a 4×8 sheet (32 sq ft) or about 48 screws for a 4×12 sheet (48 sq ft). This calculator scales the screw count automatically based on the sheet size you select, then rounds up to full boxes of 200.
Should I include the ceiling in my estimate?
Yes, unless your ceiling is already drywalled or you're using a different material. Ceiling drywall is often the same 1/2" standard board used on residential walls. Toggle the option in the calculator to see exactly how much the ceiling adds to your total.
What's the standard drywall sheet size?
The most common size is 4×8 feet (32 sq ft), available at virtually every home center. For rooms with ceilings taller than 8 feet, 4×9 or 4×10 sheets reduce the need for a horizontal seam. For commercial or large residential jobs, 4×12 or even 4×16 sheets speed up installation.
How much joint compound do I need?
This calculator estimates roughly 0.053 gallons of compound per square foot of finished drywall area, which works out to about one 4.5-gallon bucket per 80–85 square feet for two coats. Textured or skip-trowel finishes typically need 20–30% more.
How much drywall tape do I need?
Plan on roughly 0.4 linear feet of joint tape per square foot of drywall surface, which covers seams, corners, and fastener heads. That works out to about 12–13 feet per standard 4×8 sheet, in line with typical contractor rules of thumb of 12–16 ft per sheet.
How do I estimate drywall for openings like doors and windows?
Subtract the total square footage of doors and windows from your gross wall area before applying the waste factor. A standard interior door is about 18–20 sq ft, an exterior door about 21 sq ft, and a typical window is 12–15 sq ft.
