Why This Matters

Mortar is the sacrificial part of a brick wall. It is softer than the brick so it wears first, and after 25 to 40 years it often crumbles, recesses or cracks. Open joints let water into the wall, which leads to spalling brick, rotted framing, peeling interior paint and freeze-thaw damage. Repointing before that happens is far cheaper than rebuilding a wall section, which can run several times the cost.

Homeowners use an estimate like this before calling masons, so a quote of $3,000 or $15,000 can be judged against a realistic number. Buyers of older rowhouses and brownstones use it to budget deferred maintenance. Property managers use it to compare bids for a three-story façade. A 1,000 sq ft wall holds about 6,000 linear feet of joint, and a few dollars of difference per foot becomes thousands of dollars. Knowing the joint length, rather than just the wall area, makes bids easier to compare.

A quick note on terms: in the strict sense, tuckpointing means filling joints with matching mortar and adding a thin decorative line. Most contractors and homeowners use it interchangeably with repointing, and that is how this tool treats it.

How It's Calculated

The calculator works from joint length, because labor is driven by how many feet of mortar must be ground out and refilled.

  • Net area = (width × height × walls) − openings. Repointed area = net area × share of joints needing work.
  • Joint length per sq ft = 12 ÷ course height (in) + 12 ÷ brick length (in). Modular brick has an 8/3 in course and 8 in length, so 4.5 + 1.5 = 6.0 lin ft per sq ft.
  • Mortar volume (cu ft) = joint length × joint width × cut depth ÷ 144, plus 25% waste. A bag is assumed to yield 0.6 cu ft, and bags are always rounded up.
  • Labor = joint length × market rate × mortar factor (1.15 for lime). Access adds ×1.15 or ×1.40. Supplies add 5% of labor for blades, dust control and cleanup. Contingency adds 10%.
  • Total = labor + access + supplies + materials + contingency, rounded up to the next $5. Low and high are 85% and 125% of the estimate. A $1,200 minimum job charge applies to small jobs.

This follows common masonry rules of thumb: cut joints to at least 2 to 2½ times the joint width, and use mortar no harder than the surrounding brick (ASTM C270 types, and the guidance in NPS Preservation Brief 2 for historic masonry). Local codes, labor prices and contractor practices vary, so treat the output as a planning estimate. It is not a bid, an engineering opinion or an inspection.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Never use mortar harder than your brick. Portland-heavy mortar on soft, old brick forces the brick to take the moisture and stress, and the faces spall. Pre-1930 buildings usually need Type O or lime.
  • Cut deep enough. Shallow cuts of 1/4 in or less fall out within a few winters. Aim for at least 2× the joint width, and always to sound mortar.
  • Don't grind with a wide blade into the brick edges. Skilled masons use narrow blades or hand chisels on thin joints, which is part of why historic work costs more.
  • Look for structure problems first. Stair-step cracks, bulging or leaning walls, and failed lintels need a mason or structural engineer before any repointing.
  • Plan for dust and lead. Grinding creates silica dust, so crews should use shrouded grinders with vacuums and follow OSHA silica rules. Painted brick or pre-1978 trim may contain lead.
  • Get three written quotes. Ask each to state joint depth, mortar type and sample-panel approval so the quotes are comparable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does tuckpointing cost per square foot?

Most jobs land between about $8 and $25 per square foot, depending on joint size, access and region. This calculator typically shows $10 to $21 per sq ft for standard brick, and more for historic lime work or tall walls needing scaffolding.

What is the difference between tuckpointing and repointing?

Repointing replaces deteriorated mortar in the joints. Traditional tuckpointing adds a thin contrasting mortar line over the new mortar to imitate fine joints. In everyday usage, contractors use the words interchangeably.

How long does tuckpointing last?

Well-done repointing with the right mortar commonly lasts 25 to 50 years. Poor mortar choices, shallow cuts or ongoing moisture problems can shorten that to less than 10 years.

How many bags of mortar do I need for 1,000 square feet?

With modular brick, 3/8 in joints and 3/4 in depth, 1,000 sq ft needs about 6,000 lin ft of joint, or roughly 25 bags of 80 lb mortar including waste. Wider or deeper joints raise that quickly, so check the joint inputs.

Can I tuckpoint brick myself?

Small areas on a ground-level wall are feasible if you cut joints cleanly, match the mortar and keep it damp while curing. Large or elevated areas are safer with a mason, because ladder work and silica dust are real hazards.

Do I need to repoint the whole wall?

Not always. If only certain areas have failed, spot repointing saves money, so use the slider to model a smaller share. Many masons recommend the whole wall when more than about 25% of joints are bad, because matching old and new mortar can look patchy.
