Why This Matters

Dirt does not occupy the same space after you dig it. A cubic yard of clay in the ground can become about 1.4 cubic yards once it is loosened and loaded, because digging breaks the soil structure and opens air voids. Ignore that and you under-order trucks, under-size stockpile areas and blow your haul budget. A 40 × 30 ft basement dug 8 ft deep is roughly 356 bank yards, but at 25% swell you are actually moving about 444 loose yards, which is the difference between 30 loads and 38 loads.

The same physics works in reverse when you import fill. Compacting soil squeezes those voids out, so a yard of loose borrow material rarely yields a full yard of finished fill. If a spec calls for 100 compacted yards behind a retaining wall, you may need 140 or more loose yards delivered. Excavation contractors, site-work estimators, landscapers, homeowners planning a pond or grading project, and anyone ordering fill or hauling spoil use these factors. Getting them right early protects schedules and prevents the all-too-common "we're short a few loads" call.

How It's Calculated

Earthmoving uses three volume states. Bank (BCY or BCM) is soil undisturbed in place. Loose (LCY or LCM) is soil after excavation. Compacted (CCY or CCM) is soil after it is placed and compacted. The calculator follows the standard bank/loose/compacted convention used in equipment-manufacturer performance handbooks, and compaction targets are normally verified against ASTM D698 or D1557 density tests. Local specifications and soil conditions vary.

Loose = Bank × (1 + Swell%)
Compacted = Bank × (1 − Shrink%)
Load factor = 1 ÷ (1 + Swell%)
Truck loads = Loose ÷ Truck capacity, rounded up

If you know the loose volume, the calculator divides by (1 + swell) to get bank. If you know the compacted volume, it divides by (1 − shrink). Shrinkage here is measured from the original bank volume, not from the loose volume. Truck loads and the planning volume are always rounded up to the next whole number, so the estimate errs on the side of having enough capacity rather than too little.

Worked Example

You are excavating a basement 40 ft × 30 ft × 8 ft deep in common earth.

  • Bank volume: 40 × 30 × 8 = 9,600 ft³ ÷ 27 = 355.56 yd³.
  • Swell: 25% gives a swell factor of 1.25, so loose volume = 355.56 × 1.25 = 444.45 yd³. The load factor is 1 ÷ 1.25 = 0.800.
  • Trucks: with 12 yd³ loose capacity, 444.45 ÷ 12 = 37.04, which rounds up to 38 loads.
  • Compacted: with 12% shrinkage, 355.56 × 0.88 = 312.89 yd³ of soil if that same material were placed and compacted.

Enter 355.56 as bank volume with those settings and the tool shows the same figures. Now reverse it: to build 100 compacted yards of fill, divide by 0.88 to get 113.64 bank yards, which equals 142.05 loose yards to deliver.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Don't mix up volume states. Quarry and landscape suppliers sell loose yards; engineers' plans usually show bank or compacted yards. Always confirm which one a number refers to.
  • Treat the presets as starting points. Moisture, rock content and gradation move swell by 10 points or more. Use test data or local experience where you can.
  • Remember truck capacity is "struck" versus "heaped." Enter the realistic loose capacity you actually haul, not the advertised maximum, and consider legal weight limits for dense, wet soil.
  • Add contingency. Over-excavation, trench slopes, soft spots and spillage all add volume. Many estimators add 5–10% on top of the computed figure.
  • Don't confuse shrink with swell. Shrinkage is measured against the bank volume. Reversing the percentages will give you the wrong amount of fill.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a soil swell factor?

It is the ratio by which soil increases in volume when excavated, shown as a percentage. A 25% swell means 1 bank yard becomes 1.25 loose yards. It depends on soil type, moisture and how finely the material is broken up.

What is the difference between swell factor and load factor?

Swell factor is the percentage volume increase (1 + swell%), while load factor is its reciprocal, 1 ÷ (1 + swell%). Load factor converts loose volume back to bank volume. For 25% swell the load factor is 0.800.

How do I convert bank cubic yards to loose cubic yards?

Multiply the bank volume by (1 + swell%). For 200 bank yards of clay at 40% swell, 200 × 1.40 = 280 loose yards. Select "Bank" in the calculator and enter 200 to see this directly.

What is the shrink factor for compacted soil?

Shrink factor is compacted volume divided by bank volume, which is 1 minus the shrinkage percentage. A 12% shrinkage gives a factor of 0.88, so 100 bank yards compact to about 88 yards. Required compaction level, set by your project spec, strongly affects the result.

How many truck loads do I need to haul my excavated soil?

Convert the bank volume to loose volume, then divide by the truck's loose capacity and round up. For 444.45 loose yards and a 12-yard truck, you need 38 loads. Add extra for contingency if conditions are uncertain.

Is this calculator suitable for engineering or permit approval?

No. It is a planning estimate based on typical values and standard formulas. Confirm swell, shrinkage and compaction requirements with your geotechnical report, project specifications and local authorities.
