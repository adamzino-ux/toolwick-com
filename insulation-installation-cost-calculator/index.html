How to Use This Insulation Installation Cost Calculator

Pick where the insulation goes, enter the area and subtract windows, doors or other openings. Choose your climate zone and the tool suggests a target R-value, which you can adjust with the slider. Enter any insulation already in place, then choose a material and whether you or a pro will install it. The result shows total cost, a likely price range, the thickness to install, the amount of material to order and a side-by-side comparison of every insulation type.

Why This Matters

Insulation is one of the few home upgrades that pays you back every month. The U.S. Department of Energy estimates that air sealing and adding insulation can cut heating and cooling bills by about 15 percent on average. Contractor quotes for the same attic can differ by $1,500 or more, because they bundle removal, air sealing, and material markup differently. Knowing a realistic number lets you tell a fair quote from an inflated one.

The calculator suits several situations. A homeowner with a 1,200 sq ft attic at R-11 in Atlanta needs to know whether blown-in cellulose at roughly $2.50 per square foot beats fiberglass batts. A buyer budgeting for a 1950s ranch needs to compare closed-cell foam in 2x4 walls against dense-pack cellulose. A landlord insulating a floor over a crawl space wants the cheapest option that meets code. Because the tool also shows the depth each material needs, you can spot when a product will not fit your joists or studs before you buy it.

How It's Calculated

The tool works out the R-value you still need, converts it to a thickness for each material, then prices that thickness.

Needed R = Target R − Existing R
Thickness (in) = Needed R ÷ R per inch, rounded UP to the next 0.5 in
Material = thickness × $/sq ft/in × net area × (1 + waste%) × price level
Labor = (base + per-inch rate × thickness) × application factor × net area × price level
Total = (material + labor + removal + air sealing) × 1.10 contingency

Rounding thickness up means the installed R-value always meets or beats your target, never falls short. Application factors raise labor for harder work: walls ×1.3, roof decks ×1.4, crawl spaces ×1.2, floors ×1.15, attics ×1.0. DIY drops labor but adds $75 for rental and protective gear. DIY spray foam kits also cost about 1.6 times bulk rig pricing. Removal runs $1.50 per sq ft for pros and $0.40 DIY, and air sealing runs $0.60 for pros and $0.15 DIY. The likely range is 85% to 125% of the estimate. Suggested targets follow commonly cited values from 2021 IECC Table R402.1.3, using the higher figure where options exist. Worked Example

A homeowner in climate zone 3 has a 1,200 sq ft attic floor with R-11 insulation. The suggested target is R-49. They choose professionally installed blown-in cellulose, average local prices, 10% waste and air sealing. These are the tool's default inputs.

  • Needed R: 49 − 11 = 38.
  • Thickness: 38 ÷ 3.5 = 10.86 in, rounded up to 11.0 in. That adds R-38.5, for a final R-49.5.
  • Material: 11 × $0.08 × 1,200 × 1.10 = $1,161.60. You would order 1,210 cu ft.
  • Labor: ($0.45 + $0.03 × 11) × 1,200 = $936.00.
  • Air sealing: $0.60 × 1,200 = $720.00.
  • Subtotal: $2,817.60, plus 10% contingency of $281.76.
  • Total: $3,099, or about $2.58 per sq ft, with a likely range of $2,634 to $3,874.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Air seal first. Sealing gaps around wiring, plumbing and attic hatches before adding insulation often delivers more comfort per dollar than extra R-value.
  • Check cavity depth. A 2x4 wall holds 3.5 in and a 2x6 holds 5.5 in. If the result exceeds the depth, switch to a higher R-per-inch material or add continuous rigid foam.
  • Don't compress batts. Squashing R-30 into a shallow space reduces its real R-value, so buy for the depth you have.
  • Keep vents and fixtures clear. Maintain ventilation baffles at the eaves and keep insulation away from flues and non-IC-rated recessed lights.
  • Get three quotes. Ask each contractor to list removal, air sealing and R-value separately so you can compare them against this estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to insulate a house?

Most projects run $1.50 to $4.00 per square foot installed. A 1,200 sq ft attic with blown-in insulation typically costs $1,800 to $3,900. Wall retrofits and spray foam cost more because the labor is harder.

What R-value do I need for my attic?

The 2021 IECC calls for roughly R-30 in zone 1, R-49 in zones 2 and 3, and R-60 in zones 4 through 8. Your local code may differ, so check with your building department.

Is blown-in or batt insulation cheaper?

For attic floors, blown-in cellulose and fiberglass are usually cheaper per R-value and fill gaps better. Batts can be cheaper for DIY work in standard-depth wall cavities. Use the comparison table to see both for your numbers.

Can I install insulation myself?

Batts and attic loose-fill are common DIY jobs if you wear a respirator, goggles and gloves. Spray foam and dense-pack wall work are best left to professionals. Foam needs proper ventilation, and loose-fill in walls needs special equipment.

Why does the tool round the thickness up?

Rounding up to the next half inch means the installed R-value meets or exceeds your target. It costs slightly more but keeps you on the safe side of code minimums.

Does spray foam need a fire barrier?

Yes, in most cases. The IRC (section R316) generally requires foam plastics in living spaces to be covered by a thermal barrier such as half-inch drywall. Attics and crawl spaces can have different rules, so ask your inspector.
