Why This Matters

A chimney is a dam. On a sloped roof, water running downhill hits the wide uphill face of the chimney and has nowhere to go but around the sides. On a narrow chimney that works fine, but once the face gets wide, debris, snow and ice pile up behind it. The flashing at that joint becomes the most leak-prone spot on the roof. A cricket, also called a saddle, is a small two-sided roof that splits the flow and sends it around both sides.

Model codes recognize this. The International Residential Code requires a cricket where the chimney dimension parallel to the ridge exceeds 30 inches (see IRC R1003.20 and R903.2.2, and verify the edition your jurisdiction has adopted). A typical masonry chimney with a two-flue stack is 48 to 56 inches wide, so most of them need one. Homeowners get a firm quantity before calling a roofer, carpenters can pre-cut the ridge and valleys on the ground, and estimators can price sheathing and flashing without a site visit. Getting the height wrong in either direction has a cost. Too flat and water pools. Too tall and you spend extra on lumber, metal and shingles.

How It's Calculated

The model is a standard saddle with a level ridge. The ridge starts at the chimney's centerline at height H above the roof surface, and runs uphill until it meets the roof. Each face slopes down to a chimney corner, and the valleys run from those corners to the ridge's end. With chimney width W, roof pitch p (rise per 12) and a pitch ratio k (0.5 for half pitch), the formulas are:

Cricket pitch c = p × k (rise per 12)
Ridge height H = (W ÷ 2) × c ÷ 12
Ridge run R = k × (W ÷ 2)
Valley length V = √[(W÷2)² + R² + H²]
Area, both faces = R × √[H² + (W÷2)²] ÷ 144 (sq ft)

Because the ridge is level, its run depends only on the ratio k and the chimney width, not on the roof pitch. Lengths are rounded up to the next 1/8 inch, lumber to the next even foot, sheets of 4×8 sheathing up to the next whole sheet, and flashing adds a 10% lap allowance. The tool follows this geometric rule of thumb and the IRC's 30-inch trigger. Local codes vary, and this is not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A brick chimney is 48 inches wide on a 6:12 roof, and you pick a half-pitch cricket with 15% waste.

  • Cricket pitch: 6 × 0.5 = 3:12, a face angle of about 14.0°.
  • Ridge height: (48 ÷ 2) × 3 ÷ 12 = 6.00 inches.
  • Ridge run: 0.5 × 24 = 12.00 inches.
  • Valley length: √(24² + 12² + 6²) = √756 ≈ 27.50 inches, or 2′ 3½″.
  • Sheathing: 12 × √(6² + 24²) ÷ 144 = 12 × 24.74 ÷ 144 ≈ 2.06 sq ft. With 15% waste that is 2.37 sq ft, so one 4×8 sheet.
  • Valley flashing: 2 × 27.50 in × 1.10 ÷ 12 ≈ 5.04 ft, rounded up to 6 ft. Back flashing: (48 + 12) ÷ 12 = 5 ft.

That matches what the calculator outputs for these inputs.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure the right face. Use the chimney width parallel to the eave, not the depth running up the slope. Mixing them up changes every result.
  • Watch the cricket pitch. On a low-slope roof, half pitch can fall below 2:12. Asphalt shingles are not permitted under 2:12 (IRC R905.2.2), and slopes under 4:12 call for special underlayment. Use the comparison table to pick a steeper option, or cover the cricket in sheet metal or membrane.
  • Metal is often the best covering. Many roofers cap crickets in soldered or sealed sheet metal because valleys on tiny shingled saddles are hard to seal.
  • Don't skimp on flashing. Counterflash the cricket-to-chimney joint in the same way as any roof-to-wall intersection. Flashing mistakes cause most chimney leaks, far more than the cricket's geometry.
  • Verify on the roof. Chimneys and framing are rarely perfectly square. Dry-fit before final cuts, and check the plan with your roofer or building department.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a roof cricket?

A roof cricket, or saddle, is a small peaked structure built on the uphill side of a chimney or other wide penetration. It diverts water and debris around the obstruction instead of letting it collect behind it.

When is a roof cricket required?

The IRC calls for one when the chimney's dimension parallel to the ridge is more than 30 inches. Local amendments can change that, so check with your building department. Even for narrower chimneys, a cricket can help in snowy climates.

How tall should a roof cricket be?

A common rule is to make the cricket pitch half the roof pitch, so the ridge height is the chimney width times the roof pitch ÷ 48. A 48-inch chimney on a 6:12 roof gives a 6-inch ridge. Go steeper for heavy snow or low-pitch roofs.

Can I use shingles on a cricket?

Yes, if the cricket pitch is at least 2:12 and underlayment rules for the slope are followed. Below that, use sheet metal or a low-slope membrane. Many installers choose metal anyway because the valleys are very short.

Does roof pitch change the cricket ridge length?

No. With a level ridge, the run equals the pitch ratio times half the chimney width. Roof pitch only changes how tall the ridge is.

Is this calculator a substitute for a roofer or an engineer?

No. It gives geometry and material estimates based on a rule of thumb. Your local code, the actual framing, and a qualified roofer or engineer decide the final design.
