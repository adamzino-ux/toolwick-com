How to Use This Cinder Block Wall Calculator

Enter the wall length and height in feet, subtract the area of any doors or windows, then choose the block face, wall thickness, mortar joint and waste allowance. If the wall will be reinforced, pick a vertical rebar spacing and bar size. The calculator returns blocks, mortar bags, grout bags and rebar sticks to buy, plus a breakdown table and a rebar-spacing comparison.

Why This Matters

Ordering the wrong number of blocks is one of the most common masonry mistakes. Run short and you pay a second delivery fee, risk a color or batch mismatch, and leave a half-built wall open to weather. Over-order and you haul leftovers: a standard 8-inch block weighs roughly 35 to 40 lb, so 50 spare blocks is nearly a ton of material. A 20 ft × 8 ft wall is 160 sq ft, which needs 180 blocks laid and about 198 with a 10% waste allowance. Shorten the same wall to 15 ft and you need about 149. Garden walls, retaining walls, garage and workshop partitions, property-line walls and basement dividers all start with this takeoff, as do contractors pricing bids and homeowners comparing DIY cost against a mason's quote. The reinforcement numbers matter even more, because grout and rebar are what let stacked blocks resist wind, soil pressure and impact. Treat this as a planning estimate. Final design, footing depth and reinforcement must follow your local code and, for tall or retaining walls, a licensed engineer.

How It's Calculated

A nominal "16 × 8" block is actually 15⅝" × 7⅝". The nominal size already includes a 3/8" mortar joint, so the calculator adds the joint to the actual block size and never counts it twice.

Module area = (actual length + joint) × (actual height + joint)
Net blocks = (wall area − openings) × 144 ÷ module area
Total blocks = net blocks × (1 + waste %), rounded up

With a 3/8" joint a 16 × 8 block has a 128 sq in module, which is 0.889 sq ft or 1.125 blocks per sq ft. A 1/2" joint gives slightly fewer blocks per sq ft, and a 1/4" joint slightly more.

Mortar. Block is bedded on its face shells. Volume per block = joint × bedded width × (module length + actual height). Bedded width is two face shells (the full width for 4" units). The result is doubled to allow for droppings, furrowing and mixing loss, then divided by 0.45 cu ft per 60 lb bag. This works out to roughly 15 to 20 standard blocks per bag, and joint thickness changes the answer directly.

Grout. Only cores that hold rebar are grouted, so grout is zero when you choose "No rebar". Grouted cells = wall length ÷ spacing, rounded up, plus one for the end bar. Each cell's volume = core area × laid height, where core area ≈ (actual thickness − 2 face shells) × 5.1 sq in. A 10% spill allowance is added and the total is divided by 0.6 cu ft per 80 lb bag. Check your bag's printed yield.

Rebar. Bar length = laid height + a lap or footing-embed allowance of 48 bar diameters. Bars are priced in 10 ft sticks, with extra sticks for splices on tall walls.

Rounding and codes. Every quantity rounds up, and the wall height rounds up to whole courses. Reinforcement layouts follow general practice consistent with the IRC R606 masonry provisions and TMS 402, but local codes, seismic and wind zones, and soil conditions vary. This tool is not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A 20 ft × 8 ft wall, 8" thick 16 × 8 block, 3/8" joint, no openings, 10% waste, #4 rebar every 16":

  1. Area: 20 × 8 = 160 sq ft, with no openings deducted.
  2. Net blocks: 160 × 144 ÷ 128 = 180 blocks. That is 12 courses of 15 blocks.
  3. With waste: 180 × 1.10 = 198 blocks to buy.
  4. Mortar: 0.375 × 2.5 × (16 + 7.625) = 22.15 cu in per block, doubled to 44.30. Times 198 gives 8,771 cu in, or 5.08 cu ft. Divided by 0.45 that is 11.3, so buy 12 bags (60 lb).
  5. Rebar: 240" ÷ 16" = 15, plus 1 end bar = 16 bars. Each is 8 ft + 2 ft lap = 10 ft, so 16 sticks (160 linear ft).
  6. Grout: core area (7.625 − 2.5) × 5.1 = 26.14 sq in. Each cell holds 26.14 ÷ 144 × 8 = 1.452 cu ft. Sixteen cells give 23.23 cu ft, plus 10% is 25.56 cu ft (0.95 cu yd). Divided by 0.6 that is 42.6, so buy 43 bags (80 lb).

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Plan the footing separately. This tool covers the wall above the footing. Footing width, depth below frost line and dowels come from local code.
  • Design heights in whole courses. A 16 × 8 wall rises 8" per course, so a 7 ft 10 in wall is built as 8 ft. The calculator uses the laid height for rebar and grout.
  • Don't forget lintels, caps and bond beams. Openings need lintels, and many walls need a grouted bond beam or cap course. None of these are included in the totals.
  • Grout in lifts and consolidate. Tall cells are normally filled in lifts of about 4 ft and rodded or vibrated. Follow your local code and the grout manufacturer.
  • Buy from one batch. Round up to full pallets, and keep the whole wall on the same production lot to avoid visible color shifts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cinder blocks do I need per square foot?

A standard 16" × 8" block has a 128 sq in module with its mortar joint, so it covers 0.889 sq ft of wall. That is 1.125 blocks per sq ft. A 16" × 4" block needs 2.25 per sq ft.

What's the difference between a cinder block and a concrete block?

True cinder blocks used coal ash as aggregate and are rarely made today. "Cinder block" is now a common name for concrete masonry units (CMUs). For ordering and calculating, you treat them the same.

How much mortar do I need for a block wall?

Expect roughly one 60 lb bag for every 15 to 20 standard blocks, depending on joint thickness and how neatly you work. This calculator uses the block's size and joint thickness, with a generous allowance for droppings and mixing loss, and always rounds up.

Do I need rebar and grout in a cinder block wall?

Low decorative walls are sometimes built unreinforced. Taller, load-bearing, retaining and wind-exposed walls normally need vertical rebar in grouted cores, with spacing set by code or an engineer. Always confirm with your local building department.

How many blocks come on a pallet?

It varies by maker and size. A pallet of 8" blocks commonly holds somewhere around 72 to 90, and thinner blocks pack more. Ask your supplier, then round your total up to whole pallets.

Does this include the footing or concrete slab?

No. The calculator covers blocks, mortar, grout and vertical rebar for the wall only. Footings, horizontal bond-beam steel, lintels and caps must be planned separately, for example with the concrete wall calculator.
