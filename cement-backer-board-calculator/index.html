Why This Matters

Cement backer board is the stable, water-tolerant base under tile in bathrooms, kitchens, entryways and showers. Wood and drywall swell, soften and let tile pop loose when they get wet, while cement board keeps its shape. Running short mid-project is more than annoying: a 3×5 ft sheet of 1/2 in. board often costs $12 to $20 and weighs around 35 to 45 lb, so a second trip to the store costs time and money.

Ordering too much is wasteful, and ordering by square footage alone can leave you short. A 10×8 ft bathroom floor is 80 sq ft, which sounds like 5.3 sheets of 3×5 board. Once you fit full sheets to the room you need 6, and cuts around a toilet flange or tub can push that higher. This is the situation the calculator is built for: remodelers pricing a job, DIYers planning a weekend, and tile setters confirming a material order.

It also helps with the small stuff that stalls a job: roughly 250 screws for that bathroom, a roll of alkali-resistant mesh tape and two bags of thinset. Because every figure is rounded up, you end up with a little extra rather than a shortage.

How It's Calculated

The tool runs two checks and uses the larger result, so it never under-orders:

1. Area method: sheets = ceil( net area × (1 + waste %) ÷ sheet area ).

2. Layout method: the sheet is fitted to the room in both orientations. Pieces across = ceil(length ÷ piece length) and pieces down = ceil(width ÷ piece width). The orientation with fewer sheets wins. This assumes offcuts are not reused, which is the cautious assumption.

Screws: 144 ÷ spacing² gives screws per sq ft (2.25 at 8 in.). That is multiplied by the area including waste, plus 10% for edges and mistakes, then rounded up to the next 50.

Seam tape: seam length = (pieces across − 1) × width + (pieces down − 1) × length, plus 10%, divided into 50 ft rolls and rounded up.

Thinset: floors and countertops need a bedding layer under the board. The tool assumes a conservative 70 sq ft per 50 lb bag with a 1/4 in. square-notch trowel, applied to the area including waste. Wall board is screwed to studs, so no bedding is counted. Thinset for setting the tile itself is a separate purchase.

Product standards for these boards include ANSI A118.9 and ASTM C1288/C1325, and the IRC (R702.4) recognizes them as tile backers. The Tile Council of North America (TCNA) Handbook describes the assembly methods. Your manufacturer's instructions and local code take priority, and this tool is a quantity estimate, not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A 10 ft × 8 ft bathroom floor, 3×5 ft sheets, 1/2 in. board, 10% waste, 8 in. screw spacing and $14.50 per sheet:

  • Net area = 10 × 8 = 80 sq ft. With 10% waste = 88 sq ft. One sheet = 15 sq ft.
  • Area method: 88 ÷ 15 = 5.87, rounded up to 6 sheets.
  • Layout method: with the 5 ft side along the 10 ft length, 2 pieces across × 3 down (8 ÷ 3 = 2.67) = 6 sheets. The other orientation needs 8. Best is 6.
  • Buy 6 sheets (90 sq ft covered, a 12.5% effective overage). Cost: 6 × $14.50 = $87.00.
  • Screws: 88 × 2.25 = 198, plus 10% = 218, rounded up to 250.
  • Seam tape: (2 − 1) × 8 + (3 − 1) × 10 = 28 ft, plus 10% = 31 ft, so 1 roll.
  • Thinset: 88 ÷ 70 = 1.26, so 2 bags of 50 lb.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Follow the manufacturer's fastening schedule. Use corrosion-resistant cement-board screws, drive them flush without breaking the surface, and keep them the specified distance from edges and corners.
  • Bed floor board in thinset. Skipping the thinset layer under floor board leaves voids that can crack tile. Offset board seams from subfloor seams.
  • Protect your lungs. Cutting cement board creates silica-containing dust. Score and snap where possible, cut outdoors or wet, and wear an N95 or better respirator and eye protection.
  • Remember it is not waterproof. In showers and tub surrounds, add a waterproofing membrane or system rated for the application.
  • Check floor stiffness and height. Joists that flex too much crack tile, and TCNA guidance commonly cites L/360 for ceramic and L/720 for natural stone. Board, thinset and tile also raise the floor, so check door and transition clearances.
  • Calculate each room or wall separately. Cut waste does not carry from one area to the next, so adding separate results is safer than measuring one combined area.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many sheets of cement board do I need for a 100 sq ft floor?

With 3×5 ft sheets (15 sq ft each) and 10% waste you need at least 8 sheets (110 ÷ 15 = 7.33, rounded up). The exact number depends on room shape, so enter your length and width to run the layout check too.

Do I need thinset under cement board?

On floors, manufacturers generally require a bed of modified thinset between the subfloor and the board, plus screws. On walls the board is screwed to the studs and normally needs no thinset behind it. Always confirm in your product's instructions.

How many screws are in a sheet of cement board?

At 8 in. spacing, about 2.25 screws go in each square foot, so a 3×5 ft sheet takes roughly 34 to 40 once edges are included. The calculator adds a 10% cushion and rounds up to full boxes of 50.

How much waste should I allow for cement board?

Ten percent suits simple rectangular rooms. Use 15% or more for small rooms with many cuts, diagonal layouts, or irregular shapes with tubs, toilets and cabinets.

Is cement board waterproof?

No. It resists water damage and will not rot, but moisture can pass through it. In wet areas such as showers, add a waterproofing membrane or a system designed for the job.

Can I use 1/4 in. board on walls?

Thin 1/4 in. board is mostly intended for floors and countertops over a solid base. Walls and shower surrounds are usually done with 1/2 in. board. Check your manufacturer's data sheet before choosing.
