Why This Matters

Drywall is bulky, heavy and usually delivered once. Order too few sheets and you pay for a second delivery or a pickup-truck run, and a single 4×8 sheet weighs around 50–60 pounds for half-inch board. Order too many and you carry a stack of unused board out of the house. On a typical 14×12 ft bedroom with 8 ft ceilings the difference between a careful estimate and a guess is easily three or four sheets, which is $50–$75 in material and an hour of hauling.

Homeowners finishing a basement, contractors bidding an addition and landlords turning over a unit all need the same thing: a fast, defensible number. Suppliers often price by the sheet with volume breaks, so knowing you need 52 sheets rather than 48 can change which price tier you land in. The estimate also drives the rest of the order. Screws, tape and compound are bought in boxes, rolls and buckets, so small errors in area cascade into small errors everywhere. A waste factor of 10–15% covers cuts around windows, outlets and corners, and it is the cheapest insurance in the project.

How It's Calculated

Wall area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Ceiling height
Ceiling area = Length × Width (if included)
Net area = Wall area + Ceiling area − Openings
Sheets = ROUND UP( Net area × (1 + Waste %) ÷ Sheet area )

Sheet area is 4 ft times the sheet length. The other materials use common trade rules of thumb and are always rounded up to whole packages:

  • Screws: about 1 per square foot of board purchased (32 for a 4×8 sheet), in boxes of 200. Code-style spacing is commonly 12 in. on ceilings and 16 in. on walls, so this count leaves a margin.
  • Joint tape: 0.4 linear feet per square foot of net area, in 250 ft rolls.
  • Joint compound: about 0.0112 gallons per square foot of net area, or roughly 400 sq ft of board per 4.5-gallon bucket for a full three-coat, Level 4 finish. Lighter finishes need less, and textured or Level 5 finishes need more.

These constants are planning averages, not manufacturer specifications. Always check the product label for coverage.

Worked Example

A 14 ft × 12 ft bedroom, 8 ft ceiling, ceiling included, one door of 20 sq ft, 4×8 sheets, 10% waste and $18 per sheet:

  1. Wall area: 2 × (14 + 12) × 8 = 416 sq ft
  2. Ceiling area: 14 × 12 = 168 sq ft
  3. Net area: 416 + 168 − 20 = 564 sq ft
  4. With 10% waste: 564 × 1.10 = 620.4 sq ft
  5. Sheets: 620.4 ÷ 32 = 19.39, rounded up to 20 sheets
  6. Material cost: 20 × $18 = $360.00
  7. Screws: 20 × 32 = 640, which is 4 boxes of 200
  8. Tape: 564 × 0.4 = 225.6, rounded up to 226 ft, which is 1 roll
  9. Compound: 564 × 0.0112 = 6.3 gal, which is 2 buckets of 4.5 gal

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Don't skip waste. Use 10% for simple rectangular rooms and 15–20% for angled ceilings, stairwells or many windows.
  • Use longer sheets on tall walls. With 9 ft ceilings, 4×9 sheets hung vertically avoid a horizontal seam; 4×12 sheets cut ceiling seams in long rooms.
  • Pick the right board. Half-inch is common on walls, but ceilings often call for sag-resistant ½″ or 5/8″ board, and fire-rated assemblies such as garage walls usually require 5/8″ Type X. Check your local code.
  • Measure openings honestly. A door is roughly 18–21 sq ft and a window 12–15 sq ft. If you're unsure, subtract less. Extra board costs less than a shortage.
  • Buy compound and tape by finish level. Skim coats, texture and Level 5 finishes use far more compound than a standard paint-ready finish.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many screws do I need per sheet of drywall?
Plan on roughly 1 screw per square foot of board, about 32 for a 4×8 sheet and 48 for a 4×12 sheet. Common practice is 12 inches apart on ceilings and 16 inches on walls, and this calculator's count leaves a margin above that.
Should I include the ceiling in my estimate?
Include it unless the ceiling is already finished or you're using another material such as tile or plank. Switch the option to "walls only" to see how much area and cost the ceiling adds.
What size drywall sheets are available?
Sheets are 4 ft wide and commonly 8, 9, 10, 12, 14 or 16 ft long. The 4×8 is stocked everywhere, while longer sheets are often special order and cut down on seams, but they are heavier and harder to carry.
How much joint compound do I need?
This tool assumes about 0.0112 gallons per square foot, roughly 400 sq ft of board per 4.5-gallon bucket for a three-coat finish. Textured and Level 5 finishes can need considerably more, so check the label.
How much drywall tape do I need?
Budget about 0.4 linear feet of tape per square foot of board, or roughly 13 ft per 4×8 sheet. That covers flat seams, inside corners and a little extra, and it is rounded up to full 250 ft rolls.
How do I account for doors and windows?
Add up the square footage of each opening and enter the total. The calculator subtracts it before applying waste. Small openings under about 10 sq ft are often ignored in practice, which is a safe choice because it leaves extra board.
