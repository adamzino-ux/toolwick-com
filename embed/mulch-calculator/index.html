Why This Matters

Mulch is sold two ways, by the bag and by the cubic yard, and getting the quantity wrong costs time or money either way. Order too little and you make a second trip with half-finished beds; order too much and you have a pile drying out on the driveway. A typical 12 × 8 ft front-yard bed at 3 inches deep needs just under 1 cubic yard, which is about 14 two-cubic-foot bags. At $4 to $6 a bag that is $56 to $84, while a bulk yard often runs $30 to $60 plus delivery. Past roughly 2 cubic yards, bulk is almost always cheaper and faster than hauling dozens of bags.

Homeowners use a mulch calculator before spring cleanup, landscapers use it to quote jobs, property managers use it to budget annual refreshes, and community gardens use it for pathways and play areas. It also helps with logistics: a cubic yard of wood mulch weighs roughly 500 to 800 pounds, which matters for pickup trucks, trailers and wheelbarrow trips. Knowing the numbers up front lets you compare bag and bulk prices, plan delivery, and spread mulch at the right depth for weed control without smothering plant roots.

How It's Calculated

The calculator converts everything to feet, finds the volume of a flat layer, then converts to the units sold at garden centers:

Area (rectangle) = Length × Width
Area (circle) = π × (Diameter ÷ 2)²
Volume (cu ft) = Area (sq ft) × Depth (ft)
Cubic yards = Cubic feet ÷ 27
Order volume = Volume × (1 + extra %)
Bags = Order volume ÷ Bag size, rounded up

Every purchase quantity is rounded up, never down, so you do not run short. Bulk orders are rounded up to the next quarter cubic yard. Weight uses 500 to 800 lb per cubic yard, a common range for wood chips and shredded bark; wet or dense mulch can weigh more, so use the higher figure when planning a trailer or truck load. This is a rule-of-thumb planning estimate, not a code, permit or professional landscaping approval, and local rules (for example wildfire defensible-space ordinances that limit combustible mulch near buildings) vary.

Worked Example

Say you have a 12 ft × 8 ft bed and want 3 inches of mulch, with 10% extra and 2 cu ft bags (the calculator's defaults).

  • Area: 12 × 8 = 96 sq ft.
  • Depth: 3 in ÷ 12 = 0.25 ft.
  • Volume: 96 × 0.25 = 24 cu ft, which is 24 ÷ 27 = 0.89 cu yd.
  • With 10% extra: 24 × 1.10 = 26.4 cu ft = 0.98 cu yd.
  • Bags: 26.4 ÷ 2 = 13.2, rounded up to 14 bags.
  • Bulk order: 0.98 cu yd rounds up to 1.00 cu yd.
  • Weight: about 489 to 782 lb.

At 4 inches the same bed needs 35.2 cu ft (1.30 cu yd, 18 bags); at 2 inches it needs 17.6 cu ft (0.65 cu yd, 9 bags). If bags cost $4.50 and bulk is $40 per yard, that is $63 versus $40 before delivery fees.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Do not pile mulch against trunks or siding. Keep it 3 to 6 inches away from tree trunks and house walls. Mulch "volcanoes" cause bark rot, and wood mulch against siding invites moisture and pests.
  • Do not go deeper than 4 inches. Thick layers hold too much water and can suffocate roots. Two to three inches suppresses weeds well for most beds.
  • Count existing mulch. If last year's layer is still 1 inch deep, you only need to top up the difference. Rake it loose first.
  • Check your units. Mixing feet and inches is the most common error. Enter dimensions in one unit and pick it from the dropdown.
  • Subtract big obstacles, and mind local rules. For a ring around a tree, enter the outer circle and consider the trunk gap. In wildfire areas, check local guidance on combustible mulch near buildings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of mulch are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. That is 13.5 bags of 2 cu ft mulch, 9 bags of 3 cu ft, or 27 bags of 1 cu ft. Always round up to whole bags when you buy.

How deep should mulch be?

Two to three inches is right for most flower beds and shrub borders, and up to 4 inches works for coarse wood chips on paths or around established trees. Going deeper than 4 inches can smother roots and trap moisture.

How much area does one cubic yard of mulch cover?

One cubic yard covers about 324 sq ft at 1 inch deep, 162 sq ft at 2 inches, 108 sq ft at 3 inches, and 81 sq ft at 4 inches. Use the table above or the calculator for odd-shaped beds.

How much does a cubic yard of mulch weigh?

Typically 500 to 800 pounds, and more when it is wet or made from dense material. To convert for hauling, try the cubic yards to tons calculator.

Is it cheaper to buy mulch in bags or in bulk?

Bags cost roughly $54 to $81 per cubic yard at typical retail prices, while bulk is often $30 to $60 plus delivery. For under about 2 cubic yards bags can be more convenient; above that, bulk usually wins. Enter your prices above to compare.

Should I order extra mulch?

Yes, 5 to 10% extra covers settling, uneven ground and spillage. The calculator defaults to 10%, which is a conservative allowance, and you can lower it with the slider if your beds are flat and tidy.
