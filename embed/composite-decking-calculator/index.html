Why This Matters

Composite decking is not cheap. A single 16-ft, 5.5-inch board commonly runs $50 to $120 depending on brand and product line, so a 200 sq ft deck can easily cost $1,500 to $3,600 in boards alone. Ordering by square footage and rounding to the nearest board is how many homeowners end up short. Come up one board short on a Friday and you may wait a week for the next delivery. Boards from a different production lot can also differ slightly in color, and that shows on a finished surface.

Order too many and you pay restocking fees or keep leftovers you cannot return. Contractors use a decking estimate to bid jobs, DIYers use it to set a budget before committing, and people replacing a tired pressure-treated deck use it to see whether composite fits. Layout matters as much as area. Laying boards at 45 degrees typically adds about 5 percent more waste than a straight layout, and buying 20-ft boards for a 16-ft-long deck wastes 4 feet of every board. A good estimate also counts the hidden parts, fasteners and joists, so the whole materials list is on the table before you start.

How It's Calculated

Every board covers its own width plus one gap, so the calculator starts with the board pitch:

  • Pitch = board width + gap (for example 5.5 + 0.1875 = 5.6875 in).
  • Rows = round up of (deck width in inches + gap) ÷ pitch.
  • Straight layout: each row needs floor(length ÷ board length) full boards plus a remainder piece. Remainder pieces are cut from shared boards, allowing 1/8 in per saw cut. Boards = (full boards + remainder boards) × (1 + waste), rounded up.
  • Diagonal layout: linear feet = deck area × 12 ÷ pitch. Boards = linear feet × (1 + waste) ÷ board length, rounded up.
  • Joists = round up of (length × 12 ÷ spacing) + 1.
  • Crossings (where a board meets a joist) = rows × joists. Fasteners = crossings × 1 (clips) or 2 (screws) × 1.10 spare, rounded up.

Code and limits. Deck framing and decking in the U.S. generally follow IRC Section R507 together with the board manufacturer's installation instructions and ICC-ES evaluation report, which set the maximum joist spacing for each product. This tool rounds everything up, defaults to 16 in on center, and warns when you choose 24 in spacing, or anything wider than 12 in with a 45° layout. It does not size joists, beams, footings, ledger attachment or railings, and it is not engineering, permit or inspection approval. Local codes vary, so confirm requirements with your building department.

Worked Example

A 16 ft × 12 ft deck with 5.5 in boards, a 3/16 in gap, 16 ft boards, joists at 16 in on center, a straight layout, 10% waste, hidden clips and a $60 board price:

  1. Area = 16 × 12 = 192 sq ft.
  2. Pitch = 5.5 + 0.1875 = 5.6875 in.
  3. Rows = (144 + 0.1875) ÷ 5.6875 = 25.35, rounded up to 26 rows.
  4. The deck is exactly one board long, so each row takes one 16 ft board: 26 boards.
  5. Add 10% waste: 26 × 1.10 = 28.6, rounded up to 29 boards. At $60 each that is $1,740.
  6. Joists = (192 ÷ 16) + 1 = 13, each spanning 12 ft.
  7. Crossings = 26 × 13 = 338. With 10% spare: 338 × 1.10 = 371.8, rounded up to 372 clips and 372 screws.

The comparison table agrees: 12 ft boards would need 43 boards (516 linear ft) and 20 ft boards 29 boards (580 linear ft), so 16 ft boards use the least material.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Match board length to deck length. A deck that is 16 ft long fits 16 ft boards with no waste. Boards bought a foot too long waste a foot on every row.
  • Do not use 24 in joist spacing by default. Many composite products require 16 in or less, and 12 in for 45° layouts. Check the installation guide for your exact board.
  • Plan butt joints. When the deck is longer than one board, the ends of two boards must land on separate joist faces. Double the joists or add blocking, and stagger the seams.
  • Buy all boards at once. Color can vary between production lots, so order everything, plus the spare percentage, in a single purchase.
  • Remember what is not counted. Fascia, picture-frame borders, stair treads, railings, flashing, footings and framing lumber are extra.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many composite deck boards do I need for a 12x12 deck?

With the default settings (5.5 in boards, a 3/16 in gap, a straight layout and 10% waste) a 12×12 deck needs 26 rows, which rounds up to about 29 boards. That works with either 12 ft or 16 ft boards, though 12 ft boards waste nothing on length.

What waste factor should I use for composite decking?

Use about 10% for a straight layout and 15% for a 45° diagonal layout. Add more if the deck has many corners, a picture-frame border, curves or several levels, because each one creates extra cut-offs.

What gap should I leave between composite boards?

Follow the manufacturer's installation guide. Many grooved boards used with hidden clips space themselves at about 3/16 in, and some products call for wider gaps in cold weather because the boards expand when they warm up. End-to-end gaps are usually 1/8 to 1/4 in.

What joist spacing do I need for composite decking?

Sixteen inches on center is a common maximum for straight-laid residential decking, and 12 inches is typical for 45° layouts or heavy use. A few products are rated for wider spacing. Your board's evaluation report and local code have the final say.

Should I use hidden clips or face screws?

Hidden clips give a cleaner surface with no visible fasteners and set the gap for you, but they cost more and take longer to install. Face screws are cheaper and fast, and many builders use them on stairs and the border boards. This tool counts either one.

Does this calculator include framing, railings or footings?

No. It estimates decking boards, fasteners and the joist count only. Footings, beams, joist sizes, the ledger and railings must be designed to your local code, and you should ask the building department or a licensed professional for those.
