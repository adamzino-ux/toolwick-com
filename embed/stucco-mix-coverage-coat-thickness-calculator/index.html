Why This Matters

Running short mid-wall is the classic stucco mistake. Stucco has to be applied wet-on-wet within a coat, so a missed bag count means a cold joint or a rushed supply run while your mix stiffens. Finish coat is worse: a second batch bought later can differ in color, and the difference shows as a visible patch across a whole elevation.

Over-ordering hurts too. A typical 2,000 sq ft of wall area (not floor area, which is usually much smaller) with default settings needs about 234 bags, roughly $3,500 at $14.99 a bag. A 15% error either way is over $500, plus another 1,000+ pounds to haul and store. Openings matter more than people expect. Deducting a 3×7 ft door and two 3×4 ft windows (45 sq ft) removes about 5 bags, or $75–$80, from a three-coat job. Homeowners budgeting a DIY project, contractors writing bids, and plasterers loading a truck all need a number they can trust before the first bag is opened.

How It's Calculated

Area with waste = (Width × Height × Walls − Openings) × (1 + Waste %)
Bags per coat = Area with waste ÷ Coverage per bag at ⅜″ × (Coat thickness ÷ ⅜″), rounded up
Wet volume = Area with waste × Coat thickness (in feet)
Total cost = Total bags × Price per bag

Coverage on a bag label is quoted at a reference thickness, and this tool uses ⅜″. Coverage scales linearly with thickness, so a ¾″ coat needs exactly twice the bags of a ⅜″ coat over the same area. Each coat is rounded up to a whole bag separately, so the total never falls below what you will actually use. Metric inputs are converted internally (1 m = 3.28084 ft, 1 m² = 10.7639 sq ft, 1 kg = 2.20462 lb) and results are shown back in your chosen units.

For reference, the thickness checks follow commonly cited rules of thumb from ASTM C926 and the stucco provisions of the IRC (R703): about ⅞″ total for three-coat work over metal lath, made up of ⅜″ scratch, ⅜″ brown and ⅛″ finish. The calculator flags thicknesses below that and single base coats thicker than ½″. This is an estimating aid, not an engineering, permit or inspection approval. Local codes, manufacturer data sheets and your building department decide what is required on your job.

Worked Example

A single-story house has 160 linear feet of exterior wall at 9 ft high. Openings are one 3×7 ft entry door (21 sq ft), one 9×7 ft garage door (63 sq ft) and six 3×5 ft windows (90 sq ft). Use the default 3-coat system, 10% waste, pre-blended mix at 22 sq ft per 80 lb bag at ⅜″, and $14.99 per bag. Enter width 160, height 9, walls 1 and deductions 174.

  • Gross area: 160 × 9 × 1 = 1,440 sq ft.
  • Openings: 21 + 63 + 90 = 174 sq ft, so net area is 1,266 sq ft.
  • With 10% waste: 1,266 × 1.10 = 1,392.6 sq ft.
  • Bags per ⅜″ coat: 1,392.6 ÷ 22 = 63.3. Scratch rounds up to 64 and brown to 64.
  • Finish at ⅛″ is one third of that: 63.3 ÷ 3 = 21.1, rounded up to 22.
  • Total: 64 + 64 + 22 = 150 bags, or 12,000 lb. Cost: 150 × $14.99 = $2,248.50.
  • Wet volume: 1,392.6 × (⅞″ ÷ 12) = 101.54 cu ft, about 3.76 cu yd.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Use the coverage printed on your bag. Products range from about 20 to 30 sq ft per 80 lb bag at ⅜″. A wrong rate is the biggest source of error, and the defaults here lean toward lower coverage, which means more bags.
  • Measure wall height from the weep screed to the top of the wall, including gable ends and parapets. Calculating a gable as a rectangle overstates it, so add triangles separately as extra walls.
  • Buy all finish coat at once. Buy the whole finish coat from the same batch number, and mix it in the same way every time, to keep the color uniform.
  • Don't over-thicken to save a coat. A base coat much beyond ½″ in one pass is prone to sagging and shrinkage cracks. Let each coat cure as the manufacturer directs before applying the next.
  • Raise the waste allowance for detailed work. Quoins, arches, soffits and many corners all use more mix than flat-wall math suggests. Add 15–25% for them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of stucco do I need for 1,000 sq ft of wall?

With default settings (three coats, 10% waste, 22 sq ft per 80 lb bag at ⅜″) you need about 117 bags: 50 scratch, 50 brown and 17 finish. That is roughly $1,750 at $14.99 a bag. Change the coverage rate to match your product and the count updates.

How many square feet does a bag of stucco cover?

An 80 lb bag of base coat usually covers 20–30 sq ft at ⅜″ thick, and proportionally more at thinner coats. Site-mixed blends tend to yield slightly less than pre-blended products. Always use the figure on your specific bag.

What thickness should each stucco coat be?

A common three-coat system over lath is ⅜″ scratch, ⅜″ brown and ⅛″ finish, totaling about ⅞″. Two-coat and one-coat systems vary by manufacturer and base. Check the product data sheet and your local code, since this calculator only flags thicknesses well outside typical ranges.

How much waste should I add to a stucco estimate?

Plan for 10% on plain, flat walls and 15–25% on walls with many corners, arches, trim or textures. Material is lost to the hawk, to mix that stiffens before use, and to uneven substrates. Unopened bags can often be returned, so it is safer to slightly overbuy than to run out.

Can I use this for EIFS or acrylic finishes?

This tool is built for cement-based stucco sold by the bag. EIFS base coats and acrylic finishes are sold in pails and have their own coverage rates, so follow the manufacturer's figures instead. You can still use the 1-coat option with the pail's coverage if you convert it to a per-container rate.

Does the calculator work in metric?

Yes. Choose meters and kilograms in the Units menu, and enter dimensions in meters and openings in square meters. Bag weight and coverage switch to kg and m² per bag, and results are reported in m², m³ and kg.
