Why This Matters

A French drain is a gravel-filled trench holding a perforated pipe that intercepts groundwater and carries it away from a foundation, retaining wall or soggy yard. It is one of the most common drainage fixes, and also one of the easiest to under-order or dig wrong. A 100-foot drain that is 12 inches wide and 30 inches deep on average holds roughly 8 cubic yards of stone, which is about 12 tons. That is a full truckload, and a second delivery for a short order can cost $75 to $150 before you buy any extra stone.

Slope matters as much as materials. Every foot of run needs a little fall, so a long drain gets deeper at the outlet end. A drain that starts 24 inches deep and falls 1% over 100 feet ends 36 inches deep, which changes your excavation, your spoil pile, and the fabric roll width you need. Homeowners, landscapers and small contractors use a french drain calculator to price jobs, order the right amount of stone, and decide whether a deep trench is realistic for a DIY weekend or needs a machine and a pro.

How It's Calculated

The calculator treats the trench as a long rectangular box whose floor slopes evenly from the start to the outlet.

Fall (in) = Length (ft) × 12 × Slope %/100
Deep-end depth = Start depth + Fall
Average depth = Start depth + Fall ÷ 2
Gravel (yd³) = Length × (Width ÷ 12) × ((Avg depth − Cap) ÷ 12) ÷ 27 × (1 + Overage)
Gravel (tons) = Gravel yd³ × 1.4
Fabric width (in) = 2 × Avg gravel depth + Width + 12 in overlap
Spoil (yd³) = Excavation × 1.25 swell

Pipe is figured at length plus 5% for cuts and fittings, rounded up to whole 10-foot sections. Gravel is not reduced for the volume of the pipe, so you will have a little extra. Tons assume about 1.4 tons per cubic yard for washed ¾ to 1½ inch stone; ask your supplier for their exact density. Pipe capacity uses the Manning equation for full flow with n = 0.015 (corrugated pipe), rounded down. Every material quantity is rounded up, never down.

Worked Example

Say you are draining the uphill side of a house. The drain is 100 ft long, 12 in wide, starts 24 in deep, falls at 1%, uses 4-in pipe, has a 4-in topsoil cap, and you allow 10% overage.

  • Fall: 100 × 12 × 0.01 = 12 in, so the deep end is 36 in and the average depth is 30 in.
  • Excavation: 100 × 1 × 2.5 ft = 250 ft³ = 9.3 yd³. With 25% swell you haul about 11.6 yd³.
  • Gravel: average gravel depth is 30 − 4 = 26 in. 100 × 1 × 2.167 = 216.7 ft³ = 8.0 yd³, plus 10% = 8.9 yd³, or about 12.4 tons.
  • Pipe: 100 × 1.05 = 105 ft, so 11 sections or 110 ft.
  • Fabric: width is 2 × 26 + 12 + 12 = 76 in, so 100 × 6.33 ft × 1.1 = 697 sq ft. At the deep end you need an 8 ft wide roll or two overlapping passes.
  • Cost: gravel at $40/ton is $496.00, pipe at $1.50/ft is $165.00, and fabric at $0.30/sq ft is $209.10, for $870.10 in materials.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Call 811 first. Buried gas, electric and water lines are often shallower than you expect. Trenches deeper than 4 to 5 feet can collapse, so keep DIY drains shallow or hire a pro.
  • Use washed, clean stone. Crushed rock with fines clogs the fabric and the pipe. Buy washed ¾ to 1½ inch stone.
  • Don't skimp on slope. Aim for at least 1% (about 1 inch per 8 feet). Check it with a string line and level, because a flat spot holds water and silt.
  • Wrap the gravel, not the pipe. Sock-wrapped pipe can still silt up. Line the trench with fabric, add gravel and pipe, then fold the fabric over the top with a generous overlap.
  • Plan the outlet before digging. Know where the water goes (daylight, dry well or storm drain) and that it is allowed. Never discharge onto a neighbor's property.
  • Order a little extra. Gravel settles and spills. The overage field exists for that reason.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep should a French drain be?

Most residential drains are 18 to 36 inches deep. A foundation drain sits near the footing, while a yard drain can be shallower. Keep at least 2 inches of gravel under the pipe and 6 inches over it.

What slope does a French drain need?

A common rule of thumb is a minimum of 1%, which is about 1 inch of fall per 8 feet of run. Steeper is fine up to a point, but flatter risks standing water and silt buildup.

How much gravel do I need per foot of French drain?

A trench section 12 inches wide with 24 inches of gravel holds 2 cubic feet per foot of length. That is about 0.074 cubic yards, or roughly 0.1 ton of washed stone per linear foot.

Do I need filter fabric in a French drain?

In most soils, yes. Non-woven geotextile fabric keeps silt and clay out of the gravel so the drain does not clog over time. Line the trench before adding stone and fold the fabric over the top.

Should the pipe holes face up or down?

It depends on the pipe. Follow the manufacturer's markings, since some corrugated pipe is installed with holes down and some slotted rigid pipe has a specific orientation. The gravel bed around the pipe does most of the water collection either way.

Where should a French drain empty?

Ideally it daylights to a lower spot on your own property, a dry well, or an approved storm drain. Rules differ by town, so check local requirements before connecting to a street or sewer line.
