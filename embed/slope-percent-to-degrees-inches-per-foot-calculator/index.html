Why This Matters

Slope shows up in almost every build, and each trade speaks a different language. Civil drawings and grading plans use percent. Carpenters and plumbers think in inches per foot because that is what a tape measure and a 4-foot level give them. Digital levels and phone apps often read degrees. A mix-up between them is how a patio ends up draining toward the house or a ramp ends up too steep to pass inspection.

Some real numbers: a 20-foot patio that falls ¼" per foot drops 5" end to end. A 40-foot drain line at ¼" per foot drops 10". A digital level reading 1.2° looks small, but it is 2.09%, which is already above the 2.08% (¼" per foot) many drainage rules of thumb call for. Percent slope is also not "out of 90": 100% is a 45° angle, and a 12:12 roof is 100%. Converting before you cut, dig or pour keeps the numbers consistent across the plan, the level and the tape.

How It's Calculated

Every slope is a ratio of vertical rise to horizontal run. Call that ratio s (rise ÷ run). Everything else follows from it:

  • Percent = s × 100
  • Degrees = arctan(s) × 180 ÷ π (and from degrees, s = tan(angle))
  • Inches per foot = s × 12 (this is also the "x" in an x:12 roof pitch)
  • Ratio 1 : N, where N = 1 ÷ s (N units of run per 1 unit of rise)
  • Total rise = run × s, and surface length = run × √(1 + s²)

Tape-measure fractions are rounded to the nearest 1/16". Because rounding direction matters, the tool also shows the value rounded down and rounded up. Round up when you must meet a minimum slope (drainage) and round down when you must stay under a maximum (accessible ramps).

Worked Example

Say you are setting a 10-foot patio edge and want a 2% fall away from the house (these are the tool's default values).

  1. Slope ratio: s = 2 ÷ 100 = 0.02.
  2. Angle: arctan(0.02) = 1.146°.
  3. Inches per foot: 0.02 × 12 = 0.24". In sixteenths that is 3.84, so the nearest mark is 4/16 = ¼", rounded down is 3/16", and rounded up is ¼".
  4. Ratio: 1 ÷ 0.02 = 50, so the slope is 1 : 50.
  5. Total rise over 10 ft (120"): 120 × 0.02 = 2.4" (60.96 mm). Nearest 1/16" is 2⅜"; rounded up is 2 7/16".
  6. Surface length: 120 × √(1.0004) = 120.02", about 10.002 ft, so the slope barely changes the length.

The reference check shows 2% is within the ADA 1:12 and 1:48 maximums, but slightly below a ¼" per foot minimum (2.083%). If your spec says ¼" per foot, round up and use 2 7/16" over 10 ft, or better, enter 0.25 inches per foot directly.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Percent is not degrees. A 100% slope is 45°, not 90°. Small slopes are nearly equal in both (1% ≈ 0.57°), but they diverge quickly above 10%.
  • Measure the run horizontally, not along the sloped surface. Using the surface length as the run understates the slope slightly.
  • Round in the safe direction. Up for minimum drainage slopes, down for maximum ramp slopes. Rounding to the "nearest" can land on the wrong side of a code limit.
  • Roof pitch is inches per foot. A 6:12 pitch is 6 in/ft, which is 50% or 26.57°. It is not 6 degrees.
  • Check your level. Many digital levels let you switch between degrees, percent and in/ft, but verify calibration by flipping the level end for end on a flat surface before trusting it for a ¼" per foot fall.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert percent slope to degrees?

Divide the percent by 100, then take the inverse tangent (arctan) and read the result in degrees. For example, 8% becomes arctan(0.08) = 4.57°. This calculator does it automatically.

What is ¼ inch per foot in percent and degrees?

¼ inch per foot is 0.25 ÷ 12 = 2.083%, which is about 1.19° or a 1:48 ratio. It is a common minimum for drain pipe up to 2½" and for patio drainage.

What is a 1:12 slope in degrees?

A 1:12 slope is 8.33% and 4.76°, or exactly 1 inch of rise per foot of run. It is the maximum running slope for an accessible ramp under the 2010 ADA Standards.

Is a 100% slope vertical?

No. A 100% slope means the rise equals the run, which is a 45° angle. A truly vertical surface has an infinite percent slope, which is why this tool limits degrees to below 90.

How do I convert inches per foot to degrees?

Divide the inches per foot by 12 to get the rise-over-run ratio, then take arctan of that value. For 3 in/ft, arctan(0.25) = 14.04°.

What slope do I need for drainage?

Common minimums are ¼" per foot (2.08%) for small drain pipes, ⅛" per foot (1.04%) for 3"–6" pipes, and 6 inches of fall in the first 10 feet (5%) for ground away from a foundation. Your local code or the product manufacturer may require more.
