Why This Matters

Subflooring is the structural layer between your joists and your finished floor, and it is one of the easiest places to under- or over-order. A 12×14 ft bedroom is 168 sq ft, which sounds like five and a quarter sheets, but with cuts, staggered end joints and a few damaged edges you will really need six. Running short means a second trip to the lumber yard, and a second batch of panels can come from a different mill lot with slightly different thickness, which shows up as a lip under vinyl or tile.

The stakes go beyond cost. Panel thickness has to match joist spacing: a 1/2 in. panel that feels fine on 12 in. centers can bounce and squeak on 19.2 in. or 24 in. centers, and tile will crack over a floor that deflects. Contractors use estimates like these for additions, basement conversions, attic floors and sistering repairs. Homeowners use them to compare budgets before demolition starts. Getting joists, adhesive and fasteners counted up front also keeps the floor quiet, because glue plus proper fastening is what stops squeaks.

How It's Calculated

All math is done in square feet; meters are converted at 1 m = 3.28084 ft. Every rounding step goes to the safer, larger side.

  • Sheets = ⌈ (length × width × (1 + waste%)) ÷ 32 ⌉, since a 4×8 panel covers 32 sq ft.
  • Layout check = ⌈span ÷ 4 ft⌉ rows × ⌈spacing-direction ÷ 8 ft⌉ sheets per row, with panels laid 8 ft side perpendicular to the joists. This is an upper bound with no offcut reuse.
  • Joists = ⌈dimension across joists × 12 ÷ spacing⌉ + 1.
  • Adhesive: bead length = joists × span × 1.1 (10% extra for end joints). One 28 oz tube is assumed to cover 30 linear ft, a cautious figure, and tubes are rounded up.
  • Fasteners: per sheet, one fastener every 6 in. along the two 4 ft panel ends and every 12 in. along each interior joist, then ×1.05 and rounded up to the next 50.
  • Thickness check: following the IRC 2021 wood structural panel subfloor table (R503.2.1.1(1)) in a conservative way, the tool wants at least 19/32 in. (5/8 in.) panels for 12 and 16 in. joists, and 23/32 in. (3/4 in.) for 19.2 and 24 in.

The nailing rule of thumb (6 in. at panel edges, 12 in. in the field) follows the IRC fastening schedule, Table R602.3(1). Local codes vary, and this tool is a materials estimator, not engineering, permit or inspection approval. It does not verify joist size, species, grade or span.

Worked Example

A 14 ft × 12 ft room with 2×10 joists at 16 in. on center, running along the 12 ft width, 10% waste and 3/4 in. panels:

  • Area = 14 × 12 = 168 sq ft. With 10% waste: 168 × 1.10 = 184.8 sq ft.
  • Sheets = 184.8 ÷ 32 = 5.775, rounded up to 6 sheets. The layout check agrees: 3 rows (12 ÷ 4) × 2 sheets per row (14 ÷ 8, rounded up).
  • Joists = 14 × 12 ÷ 16 = 10.5, rounded up to 11, plus 1 = 12 joists.
  • Adhesive bead = 12 × 12 × 1.1 = 158.4 ft; ÷ 30 = 5.28, so 6 tubes.
  • Fasteners per sheet = 2 × 9 (ends) + 5 interior joists × 5 = 43. Six sheets × 43 × 1.05 = 271, rounded up to 300.
  • Thickness: 3/4 in. is above the 5/8 in. minimum for 16 in. centers, so it passes.

At $48 per sheet and $7 per tube, the materials total is 6 × 48 + 6 × 7 = $330.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Run sheets perpendicular to the joists and stagger the end joints by at least one joist bay, so no four corners meet at a point.
  • Leave a 1/8 in. gap at panel ends and edges for expansion, and keep panels about 1/2 in. from walls, especially in humid seasons.
  • Glue and fasten. A continuous 1/4 in. adhesive bead on each joist plus ring-shank nails or construction screws is the best squeak prevention. Do not use drywall screws.
  • Check joists first. Sight each joist for crowns and sistering needs; planing high spots is cheaper than chasing a bumpy floor later.
  • Going over tile, stone or hardwood? Ask the finish manufacturer about minimum thickness and stiffness; many call for 3/4 in. panels plus an underlayment layer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many sheets of plywood do I need for a 12x12 subfloor?

A 12×12 ft room is 144 sq ft. With a 10% waste allowance that becomes 158.4 sq ft, or 4.95 sheets, so buy 5 sheets of 4×8. If the room has closets or angles, bump waste to 15% and buy 6.

What thickness subfloor do I need for 16-inch joists?

The IRC allows 19/32 in. (often sold as 5/8 in.) rated panels over 16 in. centers, and many builders use 23/32 in. (3/4 in.) tongue-and-groove for a stiffer feel. For tile, stone or hardwood, 3/4 in. is the safer choice. Always confirm with your local building department.

Should I use plywood or OSB for a subfloor?

Both are code-approved when they carry a span rating stamp. OSB is usually cheaper and flatter, while plywood tolerates prolonged moisture exposure during construction a bit better. Pick a panel with an Exposure 1 rating either way.

Should I glue and screw a subfloor?

Yes. Construction adhesive on the joist tops bonds the panel to the framing and removes most of the movement that causes squeaks. Add ring-shank nails or screws at the edge and field spacing, about 6 in. and 12 in.

Which direction do subfloor sheets go?

Lay the 8 ft length perpendicular to the joists so each sheet spans several joists. Offset the rows so the end seams do not line up. Every panel end should land on the center of a joist.

Do I need a gap between subfloor panels?

Yes, leave about 1/8 in. between panel edges and ends. Wood panels swell with humidity, and without a gap they can buckle. Tongue-and-groove edges should be seated snugly but not hammered tight.
