Why This Matters

Curved walls show up around entry foyers, stair halls, reception desks, bar fronts and column wraps. Standard 1/2 in drywall does not bend to a tight radius on its own; force it around a 2 ft curve and the core crushes or the board snaps. Kerfing solves that. You cut a series of parallel grooves through the core on the inside of the bend, leave the paper on the outside intact, and the board folds smoothly around the framing in one dry layer, with no soaking and no overnight wait.

The danger is guessing. Space kerfs too far apart and each segment becomes a flat, so the wall reads as a faceted polygon once paint and side lighting hit it. Space them too close and you weaken the board and burn hours at the saw. A 90° curve at 24 in radius in 1/2 in board needs about 16 cuts, and each extra cut on a 96 in wall is another 8 ft of sawing. Drywall hangers use this to mark a full run before the first cut, estimators use it to price labor, and DIYers use it to avoid wasting sheets on trial and error.

How It's Calculated

Two limits control the spacing, and the tool uses the smaller one. The kerf closure limit says the groove must be able to close as the board bends. The smoothness limit says the flat segments between kerfs must stay shallow enough to hide under finish. Variables: t is board thickness, w is blade width, R is the pivot radius (the radius of the uncut paper) and f is the allowed facet depth.

Pivot radius   Rp = R (outside curve)  or  R + t (inside curve)
Closure limit  s1 = w × Rp ÷ t
Facet limit    s2 = 2 × √(2 × Rp × f − f²)
Spacing        s  = smaller of s1, s2, rounded DOWN to 1/16 in (1 mm)
Curve length   L  = sweep angle (radians) × Rp
Kerfs          N  = L ÷ s, rounded UP
Ideal kerf     w* = s × t ÷ Rp   (width that closes exactly)
Open gap left  g  = w − w*

For an outside curve the face paper is the pivot, so Rp equals the face radius. For an inside curve the kerfs sit on the visible face and the back paper is the pivot, so the board's thickness is added. Sheet count assumes 4 ft wide, 8 ft long board with kerfs running along the length.

Worked Example

A 90° outside curve, 24 in radius to the face, in 1/2 in drywall, 96 in tall, cut with a standard 1/8 in blade and a standard 1/32 in smoothness limit.

  • Rp = 24 in, since the face paper is the pivot.
  • Closure limit s1 = 0.125 × 24 ÷ 0.5 = 6.000 in.
  • Facet limit s2 = 2 × √(2 × 24 × 0.03125 − 0.03125²) = 2.449 in.
  • The smaller value governs: 2.449 in, rounded down to 2 7/16 in (2.4375 in).
  • L = (π ÷ 2) × 24 = 37.70 in of flat board.
  • N = 37.70 ÷ 2.4375 = 15.47, rounded up to 16 kerfs.
  • Total cut length = 16 × 96 in = 1,536 in (128 ft).
  • Sheets = 1 across (37.70 ÷ 48) × 1 high (96 ÷ 96) = 1 sheet.
  • Ideal kerf width = 2.4375 × 0.5 ÷ 24 = 0.051 in, so about 0.074 in of gap stays open behind each kerf, which is normal.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Kerf the inside of the bend. Kerfs close on the inside of a curve and spread open on the outside. Cut them on the wrong face and the board splits along the groove.
  • Measure the radius to the finished face. Using the stud or track radius instead shifts every answer by one board thickness.
  • Test on scrap first. Kerf a 12 in strip, bend it over a plywood template of your radius, and check the facets in raking light before cutting the full sheet.
  • Leave the face paper whole. Set the saw depth to cut the core only. If the paper tears, the segment has no hinge and that area will crack.
  • Tighten the framing. Curved track needs closer studs; many installers use 8 to 12 in on center through the curve. Fasten from the middle outward into the solid segments.
  • Do not kerf fire-rated board blindly. Removing core can void a listed assembly, so check the listing and the inspector first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far apart should I cut kerfs in drywall?

It depends on the radius, board thickness, blade width and how smooth you need the finish. For 1/2 in board on a 24 in radius with a standard blade, about 2 7/16 in works. Tighter radii and smoother finishes both shrink the spacing, which is what this kerf calculator drywall tool computes.

Which side of the drywall do I cut the kerfs on?

On the inside of the bend. For an outside (convex) wall that is the back of the board; for an inside (concave) wall it is the visible face, and those kerfs must be filled and taped during finishing. Many finishers prefer two layers of 1/4 in flexible board for inside curves.

How deep should the kerf be?

Cut through the gypsum core and stop at the face paper on the opposite side. That paper acts as the hinge, so it must stay intact. Set your blade depth to the core thickness and test on a scrap piece.

What is the tightest radius for kerf-cut drywall?

There is no single code limit. In practice the limit is the thin web left between kerfs, which the tool flags when it falls under 1/4 in. Below roughly a one-foot radius, consider layered 1/4 in flexible board or a different material.

Can I kerf 5/8 in Type X fire-rated drywall?

You can physically cut it, but kerfing removes core that the fire rating relies on. Check the tested assembly listing and talk to your inspector before doing it. Local codes vary.

Do I need to wet the drywall as well?

No. Kerfing is a dry method and the board is hung right away. Wet bending is a separate technique that needs drying time and is usually used for gentle, large-radius curves.
