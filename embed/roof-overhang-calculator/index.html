Why This Matters

Overhang looks like a small detail, but it does real work. A 12 to 24 inch eave keeps rain off siding, windows and the foundation line, which is where most moisture damage starts. A deeper overhang also shades south-facing glass in summer, when the sun is high, and still lets low winter sun in. Builders in hot, sunny climates often go to 24 inches or more, while 12 inches is common in cold, snowy areas where long tails collect ice and snow.

The number also drives money and safety. Take a 24 × 36 ft house with a 6:12 roof and 12 inch rakes. Going from a 12 inch to a 24 inch eave adds about 85 square feet of roof (two eave sides, each 38 ft long and 1 ft wider, times the 1.118 slope factor). That is a bit under one extra square of shingles, plus longer soffit runs. If you deepen the rakes to 24 inches as well, the total added is closer to 147 square feet. Longer tails are also more exposed to wind uplift and snow load. Work out the sloped length, the drop and the stock size before you buy: a rafter that finishes at 14 ft 6½ in cannot be cut from a 14 ft board, so you need 16 ft lumber and the right plan for the waste.

How It's Calculated

Overhang is measured horizontally, but rafters run along the slope. The calculator converts using the slope factor from the pitch (rise per 12 in of run):

  • Slope factor = √(144 + pitch²) ÷ 12
  • Vertical drop of the overhang = overhang × pitch ÷ 12
  • Rafter tail along the slope = overhang × slope factor
  • Full rafter length = (½ width in inches + overhang) × slope factor, measured from the building centerline (ridge) to the plumb cut at the tail
  • Plumb cut length = rafter depth × slope factor
  • Roof surface area = (length + 2 × rake) × (width + 2 × eave) × slope factor
  • Added roof from eaves (comparison table) = (length + 2 × rake) × 2 × eave ÷ 12 × slope factor, so a 0 in eave always shows 0 and your rake overhang stays fixed at the value you entered

Eave fascia is two runs of the length plus both rake overhangs. Rake fascia is four sloped runs from tail to ridge. Eave soffit area is the level soffit width times the same eave length. Ridge board thickness, birdsmouth seat, wall thickness and fascia thickness are not subtracted, so lengths err on the long side.

Results that affect what you buy or cut are rounded conservatively. Rafter lengths round up to the next ⅛ inch, lumber rounds up to the next even foot, material quantities round up to whole feet or square feet, and the birdsmouth seat limit (about one quarter of the rafter depth, a common IRC R802 rule of thumb) rounds down. The warnings reference IRC R802 (roof framing) and R302.1 (fire separation) as general guidance only. Local codes, snow and wind loads vary.

Worked Example

A 24 ft wide by 36 ft long house, 6:12 pitch, 12 in eave and rake overhangs, 2×8 rafters:

  • Slope factor = √(144 + 36) ÷ 12 = 13.416 ÷ 12 = 1.1180 (angle 26.57°).
  • Drop = 12 × 6 ÷ 12 = 6.00 in.
  • Tail along slope = 12 × 1.1180 = 13.42 in, rounded up to 1 ft 1½ in.
  • Full rafter = (144 + 12) × 1.1180 = 174.41 in, rounded up to 14 ft 6½ in. Buy 16 ft lumber.
  • Plumb cut = 7.25 × 1.1180 = 8.11 in, rounded up to 8⅛ in. Birdsmouth seat limit is 7.25 ÷ 4 = 1 13/16 in.
  • Roof footprint is 26 × 38 ft. Eave fascia is 2 × 38 = 76 ft. Rake fascia is 4 × 14.53 = 58.1, rounded up to 59 ft.
  • Roof surface = 38 × 26 × 1.1180 = 1,105 sq ft. The same roof with no overhang is 966 sq ft, so the two overhangs together add about 139 sq ft.
  • In the comparison table, the 12 in eave row shows 38 × 2 × 1.1180 = 85 sq ft added by the eaves alone, and the 24 in row shows 170 sq ft. The difference between those two rows is the 85 sq ft mentioned above.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure overhang horizontally. Many people measure along the rafter, which overstates the overhang by the slope factor. A 6:12 roof makes a 12 in horizontal overhang a 13.4 in tail.
  • Check the limit before going long. Prescriptive span tables often assume overhangs of about 24 in or less, and tails much longer than a third of the backspan can overstress the rafter. Past that, use lookouts, a ladder frame or an engineer.
  • Don't over-notch the rafter. A birdsmouth deeper than about a quarter of the rafter depth weakens the member right where it bears.
  • Add waste and check stock lengths. Order 10% extra fascia and soffit, and remember rafters come in even-foot lengths.
  • Plan for wind and fire. High-wind areas need proper uplift straps and tail blocking. Overhangs near a property line can need a fire-rated underside or be prohibited.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much roof overhang should I have?

Most homes have 12 to 24 inches on the eaves and about 6 to 12 inches on the rakes. Wetter and sunnier climates benefit from deeper eaves. Snowy regions often stay shorter because long tails collect ice dams and snow load.

How do you calculate roof overhang length?

Measure the horizontal distance from the outside wall face to the end of the tail, then multiply by the slope factor, √(144 + pitch²) ÷ 12, to get the length along the rafter. This calculator does both and also gives the vertical drop.

What is the maximum roof overhang without extra support?

A common rule of thumb is about 24 inches for ordinary rafters. Many span tables assume that figure. Longer overhangs usually need lookouts, larger rafters or an engineer's design, and a good guide is no more than a third of the backspan.

Does overhang affect how much roofing I need?

Yes. Overhang adds surface on every side. The comparison table shows how much extra roof each eave depth creates at your pitch, holding your rake overhang constant. Add waste, usually 10 to 15 percent, on top of the area shown.

Is the overhang measured to the fascia or the rafter tail?

Most framers measure to the plumb cut at the end of the rafter tail. The fascia board adds about 3/4 to 1½ inch beyond it. If you want a specific finished overhang, subtract the fascia thickness before entering the value.

Can I build an overhang near my property line?

Often not freely. Building codes such as IRC R302.1 can restrict projections within about 5 feet of a lot line, require a fire-rated underside, or prohibit them within 2 feet. Check with your local building department before framing.
