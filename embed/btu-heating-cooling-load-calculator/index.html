Why This Matters

Picking the wrong capacity is one of the costliest comfort mistakes. The flat rule of thumb of 20 BTU per square foot would give a 400 sq ft living room only 8,000 BTU/hr. It ignores the windows, the 9 ft ceiling, the people and the lamps. With those included, this calculator lands at 12,300 BTU/hr for cooling in a mixed climate with average insulation. The same room in a hot climate with poor insulation comes out at 19,110 BTU/hr, about 55% more.

Undersizing means the system runs nonstop on the hottest or coldest day and never catches up. Oversizing is just as real a problem. A 24,000 BTU/hr air conditioner in that 12,300 BTU/hr room cools the air quickly, shuts off before it removes much moisture, and leaves the space cold and clammy. Getting a ballpark before you shop helps you judge a contractor quote, choose a window unit or mini-split, or decide whether a drafty room needs insulation before a bigger heater.

How It's Calculated

This tool uses a simplified rule-of-thumb method with fixed multipliers. It does not use weather files or degree-day data. Each load is a sum of five parts:

Base = area × 20 BTU/sq ft (cooling) or 30 BTU/sq ft (heating) × insulation × climate
Windows = windows × 1,000 BTU (cooling) or 700 BTU (heating) × sun × climate
Ceiling adjustment = area × (ceiling height − 8 ft) × (base rate ÷ 8) × insulation × climate
Occupants = people × 400 BTU/hr for cooling; 0 for heating (people's heat is not credited)
Room type = a fixed adder from the notes below

The ceiling adjustment scales the base load to the room's actual volume. It is positive above 8 ft and negative below. The total is never lower than 3,000 BTU/hr, and every total is rounded up to the next whole BTU/hr. Tons are BTU/hr ÷ 12,000.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Insulation matters: in the example room, moving from poor to good insulation drops cooling from 15,000 to 10,950 BTU/hr, about 27% less.
  • Climate swings the answer: the same room needs 15,600 BTU/hr of cooling in the hot zone but 10,650 in the cold zone.
  • Treat kitchens separately: the 4,000 BTU/hr cooking adder takes the example room to 15,800 BTU/hr.
  • Don't oversize to be safe: a unit far above the load short-cycles, removes less humidity and wears out sooner. Round up one step at most.
  • Heating output is not furnace input: divide the load by the furnace efficiency (AFUE) to get input rating. Get a full Manual J before replacing a whole system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does BTU/hr actually mean?

One BTU is the energy needed to raise 1 pound of water by 1°F. BTU/hr measures how fast equipment adds or removes heat, so a 12,000 BTU/hr air conditioner removes 12,000 BTU of heat every hour. That equals one ton of cooling.

How many BTUs per square foot do I need?

A common rule of thumb is about 20 BTU per square foot for cooling. Windows, ceiling height, insulation, sun and climate can move that figure well above or below 20. This calculator applies those adjustments for you.

What size AC do I need for 1,000 square feet?

The 20 BTU/sq ft rule gives about 20,000 BTU/hr, roughly 1.5 to 2 tons. Hot climates, sunny exposure and poor insulation push that higher, and good insulation pulls it lower. For a multi-room home, enter each room on the Multi-Room tab.

Is a bigger HVAC unit always better?

No. An oversized air conditioner reaches the thermostat setting quickly and shuts off before it dehumidifies, so the room feels clammy. Frequent starts also add wear, so aim for the closest standard size at or just above the calculated load.

Is this the same as a Manual J calculation?

No. Manual J is the detailed ACCA method that contractors use, covering wall, roof and window specifications, air leakage and local design temperatures. This tool is a faster, rougher estimate for planning and for sanity-checking quotes.

How do I size a furnace from the heating load?

Furnaces are rated by input, so divide the heating load by the AFUE. A 14,900 BTU/hr load on an 80% AFUE furnace needs about 18,625 BTU/hr of input. Choose the next standard size up and confirm it with a licensed contractor.
