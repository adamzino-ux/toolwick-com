Why This Matters

Deck screws look like a trivial line item until you are standing in the store with a half-built deck and the wrong box. A 16 × 12 ft deck with 2×6 boards at 16 in joist spacing needs roughly 700 to 750 screws. That is about 14 lb of #10 × 3 in screws, which is several boxes, and the price difference between a 1 lb retail box and a 25 lb contractor box can be more than double per screw. Running out mid-project also means a second trip, and screws from a different lot can differ in head color or coating, which shows on a finished surface.

Getting the screw length right matters more than the count. A screw that only reaches 1 in into the joist can back out as boards swell and shrink through the seasons, leaving a heads-up hazard for bare feet. Homeowners planning a weekend project, contractors writing a quote and anyone comparing face screws to hidden clips all need a quick, repeatable count. Closer joist spacing, wider boards and picture-frame borders change the total, so this tool lets you test each scenario before you buy.

How It's Calculated

The calculator assumes boards run across the joists, with joists spaced along the deck length. It counts every place a board crosses a joist, multiplies by your screws per crossing, adds the waste allowance, and rounds up at each step.

boards = ceil( width × 12 ÷ (board width + gap) )
joists = ceil( length × 12 ÷ spacing ) + 1
screws = ceil( boards × joists × screws per crossing × (1 + waste%) )
pounds = ceil( screws ÷ screws per lb )
boxes = ceil( pounds ÷ box size )

Screws per pound use conservative, slightly low averages for each size, so the weight is rounded toward buying more. Actual counts vary by brand and coating, so check the count printed on your box. The penetration check subtracts the board thickness from the screw length and flags anything under 1-1/2 in. The joist spacing warning follows the IRC R507.7 wood decking table (5/4 boards up to 16 in on center, 2× boards up to 24 in when laid square to joists). Composite and PVC decking follow the manufacturer's span rating. Local codes vary, and this is an estimating aid, not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A 16 ft × 12 ft deck, 5/4 × 6 boards (5.5 in wide, 1 in thick), 1/8 in gap, joists 16 in on center, #10 × 3 in screws (about 55 per lb), 2 screws per crossing, 10% waste and 5 lb boxes:

  • Boards across the width: 12 ft × 12 = 144 in ÷ (5.5 + 0.125) = 25.6, rounded up to 26 boards.
  • Joists along the length: 16 × 12 = 192 in ÷ 16 = 12 spaces, plus 1 = 13 joists.
  • Crossings: 26 × 13 = 338. At 2 screws each that is 676 screws.
  • Waste: 676 × 1.10 = 743.6, rounded up to 744 screws.
  • Weight: 744 ÷ 55 = 13.5, rounded up to 14 lb.
  • Boxes: 14 ÷ 5 = 2.8, rounded up to 3 boxes (15 lb).
  • Penetration: 3 in screw − 1 in board = 2 in into the joist, which clears the 1-1/2 in minimum.

For comparison, the same deck at 12 in spacing needs about 973 screws, and at 24 in about 515. The 24 in figure only applies to decking rated for that span.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Match screw length to the decking. For 1 in boards, 2-1/2 to 3 in screws work. For 1-1/2 in (2×) boards, use 3 in or longer so at least 1-1/2 in reaches the joist.
  • Use screws rated for your lumber. Pressure-treated wood needs a coated or stainless screw approved for it. Cedar, redwood and ocean-side decks usually call for stainless steel to avoid staining and corrosion.
  • Raise the waste allowance for complex layouts. Diagonal boards, picture-frame borders, curves and many butt joints use more screws. Use 15% to 20% instead of 10%.
  • Do not use deck screws for structural connections. Ledger attachment, joist hangers, beam connections and guard posts need structural screws or bolts and the hardware maker's fastener. See the ledger tool below.
  • Pre-drill near board ends. Drilling within about 1 in of an end, especially on dense hardwood or composite, prevents splitting and wastes fewer boards and screws.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many screws do I need per square foot of deck?

With 5.5 in boards, a 1/8 in gap, 16 in joist spacing and 2 screws per crossing, you need about 3.2 screws per square foot. With a 10% allowance that rises to roughly 3.5. Narrower boards or 12 in spacing push this noticeably higher.

What size screw should I use for 5/4 decking?

A #8 or #10 screw that is 2-1/2 to 3 in long is the usual choice for 1 in thick boards. That gives roughly 1-1/2 to 2 in of joist penetration, which holds well. Always follow the board manufacturer's fastening instructions.

What length screw do I need for 2×6 decking?

Two-by lumber is 1-1/2 in thick, so a 3 in screw only reaches 1-1/2 in into the joist. A 3-1/2 in screw gives you 2 in of bite. A 2-1/2 in screw falls short and the calculator will warn you.

How many screws are in a pound?

It depends on gauge and length. A #8 × 2-1/2 in screw runs around 90 to 105 per pound, while a #10 × 3 in runs about 55 to 65. This calculator uses the lower figure so you buy a little extra. Check the count on your box label.

Should I use 2 or 3 screws per board at each joist?

Two face screws per crossing is standard for boards up to about 6 in wide. Use three for 8 in boards or boards that tend to cup. If you use hidden fasteners, choose 1 and follow the clip maker's screw instructions.

Can I use deck screws to attach the ledger board?

No. Ledger boards carry the deck's vertical load and need lag screws, through-bolts or structural screws specifically tested for that use, installed to the pattern in your local code. Deck screws are designed only for decking and are not a substitute.
