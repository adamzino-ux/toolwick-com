Why This Matters

Almost every decision about a pool starts with its volume. Chemical doses are printed per 10,000 gallons, so a 25% error in volume is a 25% error in every dose. Too little leaves algae and bacteria unchecked; too much can irritate skin and eyes and damage liners and equipment. Pump and filter sizing works the same way: to turn over the whole pool in eight hours, a 20,000-gallon pool needs about 42 gallons per minute through the system.

Volume also drives money and logistics. Water delivery trucks charge by the gallon, heaters are sized by how many gallons they must raise a few degrees, and winter covers, salt systems and liners are all specified by capacity. Then there is weight: water is about 8.34 pounds per US gallon, so a 20,000-gallon pool holds more than 80 tons. That matters for above-ground pool walls, decks and rooftop installations. Whether you are a new owner topping off after a storm, a buyer comparing listings, or a contractor quoting a fill, a reliable volume figure saves chemicals, water and time.

How It's Calculated

Pool volume is surface area multiplied by average water depth. The calculator finds the area from your shape, averages the depth, subtracts the gap between the water and the top of the wall, then converts cubic units to gallons and liters.

Rectangle: area = L × W
Round: area = π × (D ÷ 2)²
Oval: area = 0.7854 × L × W
Kidney: area = 0.45 × (A + B) × L
Average wall depth (sloped floor): (shallow + deep) ÷ 2
Water depth: average wall depth − water level drop
Volume: area × water depth
US gallons: cubic feet × 7.48052   Liters: cubic meters × 1,000

These are standard geometric formulas and pool-industry rules of thumb. They are not tied to any building-code edition, and local codes and health regulations vary. The kidney factor of 0.45 is an approximation, and real pools with steps, benches or a deep-end drop will differ. The sloped-floor average assumes one constant slope from shallow to deep.

The tool shows the calculated volume plus a low estimate (−5%) and a high estimate (+5%). Use the lower number for chemical dosing, so you never overdose, and the higher number for sizing fill, pump flow, heaters and covers. This is a planning estimate only, not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

Take a rectangular pool 32 ft long and 16 ft wide. The floor slopes from 3.5 ft at the shallow end to 8 ft at the deep end, and the water sits 6 inches below the top of the wall. These are the calculator's default values.

  • Surface area: 32 × 16 = 512 ft².
  • Average wall depth: (3.5 + 8) ÷ 2 = 5.75 ft.
  • Water depth: 5.75 − 0.5 ft (6 in) = 5.25 ft.
  • Volume: 512 × 5.25 = 2,688 ft³.
  • Gallons: 2,688 × 7.48052 ≈ 20,108 US gallons (about 76,116 liters or 76.12 m³).
  • Range: −5% is 19,102 gallons for dosing, and +5% is 21,113 gallons for sizing.
  • Fill time: 21,113 ÷ 9 gal/min ≈ 2,346 minutes, or about 39.1 hours on a standard garden hose.
  • Weight: about 167,800 lb, or roughly 84 tons.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure the water, not the coping. Take length and width at the inside waterline, and measure depth from the top of the wall to the floor, not from the deck.
  • Don't ignore the water level. Skimmer pools usually run 4 to 6 inches below the wall top. On a 512 ft² pool, every inch is about 319 gallons.
  • Handle flat sections separately. If the pool has a long flat shallow area, a slope and a flat deep area, average each section and weight it by its share of the length rather than using one simple average.
  • Dose in stages. Use the low estimate, add roughly half the calculated chemical, circulate, retest and adjust. Always follow the product label.
  • Check against a water meter. Reading the meter or a calibrated bucket during one fill is the best way to confirm your pool's true volume.
  • Get advice before draining. Emptying an in-ground pool can let groundwater lift or crack the shell. Ask a pool professional first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many gallons are in a 16×32 pool?

It depends on depth. A 16×32 ft rectangle averaging 4 ft of water holds about 15,300 gallons. With a 3.5 to 8 ft slope and the water 6 inches below the wall top, it is about 20,100 gallons.

How do I calculate the volume of a round pool?

Multiply π by the radius squared to get the area, then multiply by the water depth and by 7.48 for gallons. A 24 ft round pool with 4 ft of water holds about 13,500 gallons.

How do I find the average depth of a sloped pool?

If the floor slopes evenly, add the shallow and deep depths and divide by two. If it has flat sections or a sudden drop, average each section and weight it by its share of the pool's length.

Does the water level change the volume?

Yes. Each inch of water depth adds or removes surface area ÷ 12 cubic feet, then × 7.48 for gallons. On a 512 ft² pool that is about 319 gallons per inch, so use your real operating level.

How long will it take to fill my pool?

Divide the volume by your flow rate. A typical garden hose delivers about 9 gallons per minute, so a 20,000-gallon pool takes around 37 hours. Check the flow with a bucket and a timer, since household pressure varies.

Is this accurate enough for dosing chemicals?

For regular shapes it is usually within about 5%, less so for freeform pools with steps and benches. Dose from the low estimate, add chemicals in stages, and retest before adding more.
