Why This Matters

Vinyl fence panels are bulky, often special order, and rarely returnable once the pallet is delivered. Ordering two panels short on a 150 foot backyard can stall a weekend project for two weeks, while ordering two extra can tie up a couple of hundred dollars. Posts are the bigger surprise. A 6 foot privacy fence needs 9 foot posts, and every one of them sits in a hole of roughly 2 cubic feet of concrete. On a 23-post job that is over 100 sixty-pound bags, which is a truck load, not a quick hardware store run.

Homeowners use a vinyl fence calculator to compare quotes from installers, build a shopping list for a DIY weekend, or check whether switching from 6 foot to 8 foot panels saves enough posts to matter. Contractors use it for fast bids. Landlords and HOA boards use it to budget a perimeter. Getting counts right before you dig also helps you schedule utility locates, concrete delivery and permits in the right order.

How It's Calculated

The calculator works in feet and rounds every count up, never down.

Runs = corners + 1 (open) or corners (closed)
Panel length = total length − (gates × gate width)
Panels per run = ROUNDUP( panel length ÷ runs ÷ panel width )
Base panels = runs × panels per run
Order quantity = ROUNDUP( base panels × (1 + allowance) )
Posts = base panels + gates + 1 (open) or + 0 (closed)
Embed depth = largest of 30 in, half the fence height, frost depth
Concrete per hole = (π × (diameter ÷ 2)² − post width²) × embed ÷ 1,728
Bags = ROUNDUP( total concrete × 1.10 ÷ 0.45 cu ft per 60 lb bag )

Rounding each straight run separately matters because panels cannot be shared around a corner. The embed rule follows the common installer guideline that one third of the total post length should be underground, which equals half of the above-ground height, plus the minimum 30 inches and local frost depth. Vinyl fences are not covered by a single national code table, so local ordinances, HOA rules and your manufacturer's installation guide always take priority. Results are material estimates, not engineering, permit or inspection approval. Each hole also gets a 4 inch gravel base for drainage.

Worked Example

A homeowner has 150 ft of open fence line with 2 corners and one 4 ft walk gate, using 6 ft tall, 8 ft wide panels, 5 × 5 in posts, 12 in holes, 30 in frost depth, and a 5% panel allowance.

  • Runs: 2 corners + 1 = 3 runs. Panel length: 150 − 4 = 146 ft, or 48.67 ft per run.
  • Panels per run: 48.67 ÷ 8 = 6.08, rounded up to 7. Base panels: 3 × 7 = 21. With 5% allowance: 22.05, rounded up to 23 panels.
  • Posts: 21 + 1 gate + 1 end = 23 posts, with 6 to reinforce (2 end, 2 corner, 2 gate).
  • Embed: the largest of 30, 36 (half of 72 in) and 30 is 36 in, so each post is at least 9 ft long.
  • Concrete per hole: (113.1 − 25) × 36 ÷ 1,728 = 1.84 cu ft. For 23 holes plus 10%: 46.4 cu ft, or 104 sixty-pound bags (78 eighty-pound bags).
  • Gravel: about 6.6 cu ft including 10% extra.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure the fence line, not the lot line. Stake and string the exact path, including setbacks, and check your property survey before you order.
  • Don't forget the gate posts. Gates carry swinging weight, so use reinforced posts with aluminum or steel inserts and a wider footing if the manufacturer recommends it.
  • Dig below frost depth. Shallow posts heave in freeze-thaw cycles and the fence racks out of line. When unsure, go deeper, not shallower.
  • Plan each run on its own. Panels can't wrap a corner, so uneven runs create leftovers. Adjust post spacing slightly or buy a spare panel to cut.
  • Call 811 before digging and confirm permit, height and setback rules with your city and HOA before you buy anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many vinyl fence panels do I need for 100 feet?

With 8 ft panels and no gates or corners, 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so you need 13 panels and 14 posts for an open run. Each corner or gate can add a panel because partial runs are rounded up separately, so use the calculator for your exact layout.

How deep should vinyl fence posts be buried?

A common rule is one third of the total post length underground, at least 30 inches, and always below your local frost line. For a 6 ft fence that means 36 inches in the ground and a 9 ft post. Check your local code and the manufacturer's instructions, which can require more.

How many bags of concrete per vinyl fence post?

A 12 in hole, 36 in deep, with a 5 × 5 in post takes about 1.84 cubic feet, roughly 4 sixty-pound bags or 3 eighty-pound bags. The calculator adds 10% for spillage and uneven holes.

Do corner and end posts count as extra posts?

No. Corner posts replace line posts at a direction change and end posts finish an open run, so they are all part of the total. The calculator lists them separately because they should be reinforced.

Why does the calculator add extra panels?

Cut mistakes, shipping damage and uneven ground can all consume a panel. The slider adds 0 to 15% and always rounds up, so you don't have to wait on a reorder with a different dye lot.

Do I need a permit for a vinyl fence?

Most towns require one, and many limit height to about 4 ft in front yards and 6 ft in back yards. Some HOAs also restrict color and style. Check before you buy materials.
