How to Use This Deck Beam Size Calculator

Pick whether your deck is attached to the house or freestanding, then enter the joist span (ledger to beam, or beam to beam), how far the joists cantilever past the beam, and the distance between posts along the beam. Choose your lumber species, live load, dead load and treatment. The calculator returns the smallest beam that passes, the maximum post spacing for all six built-up options, the line load on the beam and an estimated load on each post.

Why This Matters

The beam is the part of a deck you cannot see failing until it is too late. It collects the load from every joist and funnels it into a handful of posts and footings. An undersized beam sags over time, cracks finishes, loosens fasteners and, in the worst case, lets a loaded deck collapse. Most serious deck failures are traced to ledger connections, but overspanned or poorly supported beams are a close second.

Consider a typical 12 × 16 ft deck with joists spanning 12 ft off the house. At 50 psf total load, the beam carries roughly 300 pounds for every foot of its length. Spaced 8 ft apart, each interior post sees about 3,000 pounds. A 2-ply 2x8 that looks fine on paper may only span about 5 to 6 ft with that load, so skipping the math can mean the wrong lumber and too few posts.

Homeowners use a deck beam size calculator while planning a build, contractors use it to price lumber and posts before bidding, and inspectors and permit counters see the same question daily. It is also useful when you want fewer footings: a heavier beam lets you stretch post spacing and dig fewer holes.

How It's Calculated

The calculator uses allowable stress design for No. 2 dimension lumber in wet service, in the style of the NDS and the load assumptions of 2021 IRC R301.5 and R507 (40 psf live, 10 psf dead). It does not copy the IRC R507.5 span tables; it computes each beam and uses deliberately conservative lumber values. Local codes and amendments vary.

Tributary width. For a deck attached to a ledger, the beam reaction per foot is w × (L + c)² ÷ (2L), where L is the joist span and c the cantilever. For a freestanding deck it is w × (L ÷ 2 + c). Multiply by total psf and add the beam's own weight (40 lb/ft³).

Three limits are checked, and the smallest governs:

  • Bending: span = √(8 × Fb′ × S ÷ (12 × w))
  • Shear: span = 4 × Fv′ × A ÷ (3 × w), with no reduction at the supports
  • Deflection: L/360 for live load and L/240 for total load, from Δ = 5wL⁴ ÷ (384 × E × I)

Design values are reduced for wet service (Fb × 0.85, Fv × 0.97, E × 0.90) and, by default, for incising (Fb and Fv × 0.80, E × 0.95). Each result is rounded down to the next whole inch. Beams are treated as simple spans, which is conservative for bending over multiple posts.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Do not notch posts to seat the beam without checking local rules. Resting the beam on top of the post with a cap connector is the strongest approach.
  • Splice beam plies over a post, never mid-span, and stagger the joints between plies.
  • Keep the cantilever within one-fourth of the joist span. Larger cantilevers need engineered framing, and the calculator will not accept them.
  • Use the grade stamp. If your lumber is No. 1, No. 2 or a lesser grade, size to the stamp, not the species. Select No. 2 values only if that is what you are buying.
  • Do not stretch a result. If the table says 8′ 8″, plan for 8′ or less, and use the next size up when the span is within 10% of the limit.
  • Remember the footing. A stronger beam concentrates more load per post, so recheck footing size and frost depth.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size beam do I need for a 12x12 deck?

It depends on the joist span and post spacing, not on the deck footprint alone. A 12 ft joist span on an attached deck puts about 300 plf on the beam, which usually calls for a 2-ply 2x12 or a 3-ply 2x10 with posts around 6 to 8 ft apart. Run the numbers above with your exact setup.

How far can a 2-ply 2x10 beam span?

With a 10 ft joist span, 50 psf total load and incised Southern Pine, a 2-ply 2x10 reaches roughly 7 to 7.5 ft between posts. Shorter joist spans and denser species such as Douglas fir stretch that further. Use the options table to see your exact figure.

Is a 3-ply beam better than a 2-ply beam?

A 3-ply beam is 50% stronger in bending and stiffer than a 2-ply of the same depth, and it also gives a wider bearing surface on a 6x6 post. It costs more lumber and needs more fasteners, so compare the next depth up (for example, 2-ply 2x12 versus 3-ply 2x10).

Can I use this deck beam size calculator for a hot tub?

You can enter 100 psf, but a filled hot tub often weighs several thousand pounds on a small footprint. Point loads like that are not covered by a uniform-load beam check, so have an engineer design the framing beneath it.

Does the beam go under the joists or between them?

A dropped beam sits under the joists and is the simplest to build and the strongest for a given size. A flush beam has joists hung from it, which gives a lower profile but requires joist hangers and a deeper beam. This calculator assumes a dropped beam supporting joists on top.

Is this result enough to get a permit?

No. It is a planning estimate based on common design values, not engineering, permit or inspection approval. Your building department may require its own span tables, and an inspector has the final say.
