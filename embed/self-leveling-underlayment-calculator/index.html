Why This Matters

Self-leveling underlayment (SLU) is a cement- or gypsum-based liquid that flows out flat over a subfloor before tile, vinyl plank, laminate or hardwood goes down. It fixes dips and slopes that would otherwise telegraph through flooring, crack tile grout, or open up seams. Contractors and DIYers use it in kitchen remodels, basement conversions, and when joining a new room to an older one with a different floor height.

Ordering the right amount matters more than with most materials. SLU has a short working time, often 15 to 20 minutes, and a pour that stops halfway leaves a visible cold joint. Running out means a second mix on a floor that is already setting. Buying too much is expensive too: a 12 × 10 ft room at 1/2 inch deep needs around 13 bags, and at $40 a bag, three spares is $120 you may not use. Thickness is the biggest swing factor. Doubling depth doubles the bags, so measuring your low spots with a straightedge and level before you shop pays off quickly.

Weight is a hidden factor as well. Poured SLU adds roughly 9 lb per square foot for each inch of depth, so a thick pour over a large wood-framed floor is real extra dead load.

How It's Calculated

The calculator uses a volume method, the standard rule of thumb for estimating SLU. No building code edition governs this estimate. The manufacturer's data sheet is the authority for yield, minimum and maximum depth, and mixing water.

Area = length × width
Volume (cu ft) = area (sq ft) × thickness (in) ÷ 12
Volume with waste = volume × (1 + waste %)
Bags = volume with waste ÷ yield per bag, rounded UP

The default yield is deliberately conservative: about 0.009 cu ft per pound of dry mix, so a 50 lb bag is 0.45 cu ft. Some products yield slightly more, but using the lower figure means you are less likely to run short. Bag counts always round up to the next whole bag. Water is estimated at 4.5 to 6 quarts per 50 lb bag, scaled for bag weight. Always follow the exact ratio printed on your bag, because too much water weakens the cured surface.

Metric inputs are converted internally (1 m = 3.28084 ft, 25.4 mm = 1 in, 1 cu ft = 28.317 L), so both unit systems give the same answer.

Worked Example

A 12 ft × 10 ft bedroom needs a 1/4 inch average pour using 50 lb bags at 0.45 cu ft each, with 10% waste.

  • Area: 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft
  • Volume: 120 × 0.25 ÷ 12 = 2.5 cu ft
  • With 10% waste: 2.5 × 1.10 = 2.75 cu ft
  • Bags: 2.75 ÷ 0.45 = 6.11, rounded up to 7 bags
  • Weight: 7 × 50 = 350 lb of dry mix
  • Water: 7 × 4.5 to 6 qt = 31.5 to 42 qt, or about 7.9 to 10.5 gallons

At $40 per bag, that is $280. If the same floor needed 1/2 inch, the answer jumps to 13 bags, which is why the comparison table is worth a look.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Prime first. Unprimed concrete or wood pulls water out of the mix, causing pinholes and weak bonding. Use the primer your SLU maker specifies.
  • Seal every gap and drain. Liquid SLU runs through cracks, pipe penetrations and gaps at walls. Foam, caulk and dams are cheap compared with a leak into the room below.
  • Respect depth limits. Most products need about 1/8 inch minimum and 1 to 1.5 inches maximum per pour. Deeper fills may need aggregate or multiple lifts. Check the data sheet.
  • Mix only what you can pour. Work with a helper, mix bag by bag in consistent water amounts, and keep a wet edge. Wear a dust mask while mixing, since dry powder contains fine silica.
  • Test moisture and check structure. Concrete slabs are often tested with in-situ RH probes (ASTM F2170). On wood floors, confirm the framing can carry the added weight and is stiff enough, because SLU is not a structural fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bag of self-leveling underlayment cover?

A typical 50 lb bag covers roughly 24 to 27 square feet at 1/4 inch deep, or about 54 square feet at 1/8 inch. Coverage drops in proportion as depth increases, so always confirm with your product's data sheet.

How thick can I pour self-leveling underlayment?

Most products allow about 1/8 inch up to 1 to 1.5 inches in a single pour. Deeper areas may need a mix with added pea gravel or a second pour after the first has cured, as the manufacturer directs.

How many bags of self leveler do I need for 500 square feet?

At 1/4 inch deep with 10% waste and 0.45 cu ft per bag, 500 sq ft needs about 26 bags. At 1/2 inch it needs about 51 bags. Enter 500 as the area (for example 25 × 20) to confirm for your own depth.

Do I need primer before self-leveling underlayment?

Yes, almost always. Primer controls how fast the substrate absorbs water and helps the SLU bond. Skipping it is one of the most common causes of pinholes, cracking and delamination.

How long until I can walk on it or install flooring?

Many products are walkable in 2 to 4 hours, and some accept tile in the same day. Moisture-sensitive floors such as wood or vinyl often need 16 to 72 hours, so check the data sheet for your specific product and thickness.

Why does the calculator add a waste allowance?

Real floors have deeper low spots than your average, some material stays in the bucket and mixer, and edges need dams. A 10% allowance is typical, and 15 to 20% is smart for badly uneven floors or first-time pours.
