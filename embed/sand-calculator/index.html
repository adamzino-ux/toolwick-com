Why This Matters

Sand is sold three different ways: by the bag at a hardware store, by the ton or cubic yard from a landscape yard, or by the truckload. Ordering the wrong amount is expensive either way. Run short, and you pay a second delivery fee, often $50 to $150, and risk a color or grade mismatch between batches. Over-order, and you are left with a pile that is hard to get rid of.

The numbers add up fast. An 8 × 8 ft sandbox at 6 inches deep needs about 1.3 cubic yards. A 10 × 10 ft paver patio with a 1-inch bedding layer needs only about a third of a yard. A 30 × 60 ft beach volleyball court at 12 inches deep needs roughly 67 cubic yards, around 90 tons of dry sand before any extra. Weight matters for logistics too: a cubic yard of damp sand weighs about 3,100 pounds, more than the payload of most half-ton pickups, so a single yard can overload a truck that looks big enough. This sand calculator turns your dimensions into volume, weight and bag counts so you can order once and haul safely.

How It's Calculated

The calculator works out the surface area, multiplies by depth to get volume, adds your extra allowance, and then converts volume to weight using the density of the sand.

Area (rectangle) = length × width
Area (circle) = π × (diameter ÷ 2)²
Volume = area × depth
Total volume = volume × (1 + extra % ÷ 100)
Weight = total volume × density
Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27
Bags = total weight ÷ bag weight, rounded up

Typical densities used here are 100 lb/ft³ (1,602 kg/m³) for dry loose sand, 115 lb/ft³ (1,842 kg/m³) for damp sand and 125 lb/ft³ (2,002 kg/m³) for wet or compacted sand. These are common rule-of-thumb values, not code requirements; your supplier's actual figure can differ by 10% or more depending on grain size and moisture.

To keep the results on the safe side, bag counts are always rounded up, the bulk order is rounded up to the next ¼ cubic yard (or 0.1 m³), and tons are rounded up to the next 0.1. Good practice references include ICPI guidance of about 1 inch of bedding sand under pavers, and ASTM C33 (concrete sand) and ASTM C144 (masonry sand) for sand grading. For playground fall zones, follow the CPSC Public Playground Safety Handbook and ASTM F1292, because required depth depends on fall height. Local codes and manufacturer instructions vary, and this tool is an estimate, not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A 8 × 8 ft kids' sandbox, 6 inches deep, dry play sand, 10% extra, 50 lb bags, $40 per ton. These are the default values in the tool.

  1. Area: 8 × 8 = 64 ft².
  2. Depth: 6 in = 0.5 ft, so volume = 64 × 0.5 = 32 ft³.
  3. Add 10% extra: 32 × 1.10 = 35.2 ft³.
  4. Cubic yards: 35.2 ÷ 27 = 1.30 yd³. A bulk order rounds up to 1.50 yd³.
  5. Weight: 35.2 × 100 = 3,520 lb = 1.76 short tons.
  6. Bags: 3,520 ÷ 50 = 70.4, rounded up to 71 bags.
  7. Cost: 1.76 × $40 = $70.40 before delivery fees.

If bagged sand costs about $5 a bag, those 71 bags would cost roughly $355, so bulk delivery is usually the better deal for anything over a few bags.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Mixing units. Depth is usually in inches or centimeters while length is in feet or meters. This tool converts for you, but check each label before calculating.
  • Forgetting compaction. Loose sand settles 10 to 15% after wetting or tamping. Use 5 to 10% for a simple fill and 10 to 15% for bedding or play areas.
  • Using the wrong sand. Play sand is washed and fine, mason sand is finer and cleaner for mortar, and concrete sand is coarse. Use the grade your project calls for.
  • Ignoring weight. A yard of sand is 1.35 to 1.7 tons depending on moisture. Check your vehicle's payload and never overload a trailer or truck.
  • Not measuring irregular areas. Split odd shapes into rectangles and circles, calculate each one, and add the results. When in doubt, round dimensions up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of sand are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. With 50 lb bags of dry sand at about 100 lb/ft³, that is roughly 54 bags per cubic yard. With damp sand it is about 62 bags.

How much does a cubic yard of sand weigh?

Dry loose sand weighs about 2,700 lb (1.35 tons) per cubic yard, damp sand about 3,100 lb (1.55 tons), and wet compacted sand about 3,400 lb (1.7 tons). Always confirm with your supplier.

How deep should sand be in a sandbox?

Most guidance suggests at least 6 inches so children can dig without hitting the bottom, and 8 to 12 inches is better. For play areas with fall heights, follow CPSC and ASTM F1292 guidance and local rules.

How much sand do I need under pavers?

Use about 1 inch of screeded bedding sand over a compacted aggregate base. Do not use it to fill low spots, because thick sand layers settle unevenly and cause dips.

How much extra sand should I order?

Add 5 to 10% for simple fills and 10 to 15% for bedding or play areas, where sand compacts and spills. This sand calculator defaults to 10%, and you can change it with the slider.

How many tons of sand do I need for 1 cubic yard?

One cubic yard of sand is about 1.35 to 1.7 short tons, depending on moisture. Put another way, a ton of dry sand covers roughly 0.74 cubic yards.
