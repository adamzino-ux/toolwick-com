Why This Matters

Building a house is the largest purchase many people ever manage directly, and the gap between a rough guess and a realistic budget decides whether a project finishes or stalls. A family planning a 2,000 sq ft home might casually assume $150 per square foot and budget $300,000. After adding a garage, a crawlspace foundation, a sloped lot, permits and the inevitable change orders, the real number is closer to $470,000. That difference is what sends people back to lenders mid-build.

This estimate is useful at several moments: when deciding whether a lot is affordable once construction is included, when comparing a ranch with a two-story of equal square footage, when sizing a construction loan, or when checking a builder's quote for gaps. Early on, a quick range lets you decide between 1,800 and 2,400 sq ft before paying an architect. Later, the category breakdown shows where your money goes, so you know whether trimming the kitchen or the garage saves more. Treat the result as a planning number, not a bid; only written quotes from licensed local contractors are firm prices.

How It's Calculated

The calculator builds the estimate in layers, then adds soft costs and contingency on top of the hard construction cost.

Base = sq ft × quality rate × region factor × story factor
Hard cost = Base + extra baths + garage + foundation + site work
Soft costs = Hard cost × soft %
Contingency = (Hard + Soft) × contingency %
Total build cost = Hard + Soft + Contingency

Quality rates are $125, $160, $210 and $310 per sq ft at national-average pricing. Story factors reflect that one-story homes need more foundation and roof per square foot (×1.08), while two-story homes are the most efficient (×1.00) and three stories add stair and structural cost (×1.07). Bathrooms beyond two add $9,000 each at the national rate. Garages add $20,000, $34,000 or $48,000 for one, two or three bays. Foundation adders are applied to the footprint (living area divided by stories): $0 for slab, $8 per sq ft for crawlspace, $32 per sq ft for an unfinished basement. Garage, bath and foundation figures scale with the region factor; site work is a flat amount.

The breakdown table allocates the hard cost to typical trade categories using fixed percentages. To stay on the conservative side, the headline total is rounded up to the next $1,000, and the range runs from 10% below to 20% above the exact figure, because overruns are far more common than savings. Costs are not tied to a particular code edition, but local adoption of the International Residential Code (IRC), frost depth, wind and seismic rules can change foundation and framing costs. This tool is not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

These are the default inputs: 2,000 sq ft, Standard quality, national average, 2 stories, 2 baths, 2-car garage, crawlspace, typical lot, 10% soft costs, 10% contingency.

  • Base: 2,000 × $160 × 1.00 × 1.00 = $320,000.
  • Extra baths: 2 baths means none beyond two, so $0.
  • Garage: 2-car = $34,000.
  • Foundation: footprint 2,000 ÷ 2 = 1,000 sq ft × $8 = $8,000.
  • Site work: $25,000.
  • Hard cost: $320,000 + $34,000 + $8,000 + $25,000 = $387,000.
  • Soft costs: 10% × $387,000 = $38,700.
  • Contingency: 10% × $425,700 = $42,570.
  • Total: $468,270, shown as $469,000 rounded up. That is $234.14 per sq ft, with a range of about $421,000 to $562,000.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Don't compare per-square-foot numbers carelessly. Some quotes exclude site work, garage, permits or utilities. Ask what is included before comparing.
  • Keep contingency at 10% or more. Soil surprises, price changes and owner upgrades regularly eat 5–15% of budgets. Cutting contingency to zero does not remove the risk.
  • Price the lot separately. A cheap sloped or rural lot can cost $30,000–$60,000 more to prepare than a level one with utilities at the street.
  • Budget for what's outside the estimate. Landscaping, long driveways, fencing, window coverings, furniture and construction-loan interest are typically extra.
  • Get three written bids. Use this estimate as a sanity check, then confirm local codes, frost depth and permit fees with your building department.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to build a house per square foot?

In the U.S., hard construction typically runs about $125 to $310 per square foot depending on finish level, before land, site work and soft costs. Once garage, foundation, permits and contingency are included, all-in figures are often 25–45% higher than the base rate.

Is it cheaper to build a house or buy one?

It depends on your market. Building usually costs more per square foot than buying an existing home, but you get exactly the layout and efficiency you want. Compare this estimate, plus land, against recent local sales of similar homes.

Does this estimate include the cost of land?

The headline build cost excludes land. If you enter a land price, the results also show a combined project total so you can see the full purchase and construction budget.

Why does a two-story house cost less per square foot than a ranch?

A two-story house stacks floors on one foundation and under one roof, so the most expensive structural elements cover more living area. A ranch of the same size needs double the foundation and roof, which is why the one-story factor is higher here.

How much contingency should I add when building a house?

Most lenders and builders recommend at least 10%, and 15–20% for complex sites, renovations or volatile material markets. The calculator warns you if you set it below 10%.

Can I use this estimate for a permit or loan application?

No. This is a planning tool, not a bid, engineering document or permit approval. Lenders and building departments require itemized contractor bids, plans and local code review.
