How to Use This Egress Window Size Calculator

Measure the net clear opening: the unobstructed hole you can actually climb through with the window fully open, not the frame or glass size. Enter its width and height, plus the distance from the finished floor to the bottom of that opening, then choose the room type. If the sill sits below outside grade, also enter the window well's width, projection (how far it extends out from the wall) and depth, then press Check. You get the net clear area, a pass or fail line for every IRC R310 limit, the smallest opening that would pass, and the well and ladder checks.

Why This Matters

An egress opening is the escape route when a stairway is blocked by fire or smoke, and it is how firefighters get in wearing bulky gear and air packs. That is why the IRC treats a bedroom window as a life-safety item, not a nicety. The check matters most when you finish a basement and add a bedroom, replace old windows, convert an attic, or buy a house with an unpermitted basement bedroom.

Sizes surprise people. A 36″ × 36″ double-hung looks huge, but only the lower sash opens, leaving roughly 36″ × 16″. That is about 4.0 sq ft and well under the 24″ minimum height. A 32″ × 36″ casement swings fully open, but hinges and sash frame still take a few inches, so it may net about 28″ × 32″, or 6.2 sq ft. Cutting or enlarging an opening in a foundation wall commonly costs several thousand dollars, so checking the numbers before you order is far cheaper than failing an inspection. Appraisers and listing agents also care: a "bedroom" without compliant egress is often counted as a bonus room.

How It's Calculated

All formulas use inches and convert to square feet by dividing by 144:

Net clear area (sq ft) = width × height ÷ 144

Well horizontal area (sq ft) = well width × projection ÷ 144

Minimum height at your width = required area × 144 ÷ width

The opening limits are checked independently, and every one must pass:

  • Net clear area: at least 5.7 sq ft, or at least 5.0 sq ft for grade-floor and below-grade openings.
  • Net clear height: at least 24 inches.
  • Net clear width: at least 20 inches.
  • Sill height: no more than 44 inches from the floor to the bottom of the clear opening.

Window wells must have a horizontal area of at least 9 sq ft with a projection and width of at least 36 inches each. A well deeper than 44 inches needs a permanently attached ladder or steps. Rungs must be at least 12 inches wide, project at least 3 inches from the wall, and be spaced no more than 18 inches apart vertically. The ladder may intrude up to 6 inches into the required well dimensions. This tool does not take that allowance, so it checks your wall-to-wall well dimensions against the full 36 inches.

Under R310.1, every sleeping room and every basement needs an emergency escape opening. The exceptions are storm shelters and basements that only house mechanical equipment and are 200 sq ft or less.

The tool rounds on the safe side. Displayed areas are truncated, never rounded up, and "smallest passing size" values round up to the next ¼ inch. The numbers follow the 2021 IRC. Recent editions are very similar, but states and cities adopt different editions and add amendments.

Worked Example

A basement bedroom has a casement window with a 32″ wide × 28″ high net clear opening, a 40″ sill, and a well 42″ wide, 36″ projection and 60″ deep.

  • Area = 32 × 28 ÷ 144 = 6.22 sq ft. That is above the 5.0 sq ft below-grade minimum, and also above 5.7.
  • Height 28″ is at least 24″, width 32″ is at least 20″, and sill 40″ is no more than 44″. All pass.
  • Well area = 42 × 36 ÷ 144 = 10.50 sq ft, which is at least 9. Width 42″ and projection 36″ both pass.
  • Depth 60″ is over 44″, so a ladder or steps are required.
  • Smallest passing sizes: at 32″ wide the height can drop to the 24″ minimum, and at 28″ tall the width needs at least 25.75″.

Result: the opening and well pass, with a ladder required.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure the open window, not the frame. Sashes, hardware and tracks all reduce the clear opening. Round your measurements down.
  • Double-hung and sliding windows lose about half their size. Check the manufacturer's published net clear opening, or choose casement or awning styles.
  • Measure sill height to the bottom of the clear opening. Measuring to the glass or frame can understate it by an inch or more.
  • Do not forget the ladder. Wells deeper than 44 inches need one, and it must remain usable with the window fully open.
  • Plan well drainage and covers. Wells must drain, and any cover must open from inside without keys, tools or special knowledge.
  • Check inside security. Bars, grilles and screens must release from the inside without tools or keys.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum egress window size?

The net clear opening must be at least 5.7 sq ft (5.0 sq ft for grade-floor and below-grade openings), at least 24 inches high and 20 inches wide, with the sill no more than 44 inches above the floor. All of these must pass at the same time. Meeting the area alone is not enough.

Why are there two area numbers, 5.7 and 5.0?

The IRC allows a smaller 5.0 sq ft opening at grade-floor and below-grade locations, because rescuers and occupants can reach those openings from the ground. Upper-floor openings need 5.7 sq ft. Some local codes apply 5.7 everywhere, so a larger opening is the safer choice.

Do all basements need an egress window?

Yes, with narrow exceptions. IRC R310.1 requires an emergency escape and rescue opening in every basement, and in each sleeping room within it. The exceptions are storm shelters and basements that only house mechanical equipment and are 200 sq ft or less. A storage basement is not exempt.

Do I need a ladder in my window well?

Yes, if the well is deeper than 44 inches. The ladder or steps must be permanently attached and usable with the window fully open. They must be at least 12 inches wide, project at least 3 inches from the wall, and have rungs no more than 18 inches apart.

Can I put a cover on an egress window well?

Yes, but it must be operable from the inside without a key, tool or special knowledge, and within the operating force the code allows. Locked or screwed-down covers are not acceptable. Choose a cover rated for egress use and confirm with your inspector.

Does a replacement window have to meet today's egress sizes?

The IRC has a limited exception for like-for-like replacement windows in an existing compliant egress opening, but it does not apply to new bedrooms or changes of use. Because details vary by jurisdiction, ask your building department before relying on it.
