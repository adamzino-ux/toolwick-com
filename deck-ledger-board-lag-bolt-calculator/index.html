How to Use This Deck Ledger Board Lag Bolt Calculator

Enter the length of your ledger board in feet, then the deck joist span: the distance the joists run from the ledger to the beam or girder. Choose the fastener you plan to use and your ledger lumber size, then press Calculate. You get the total fastener count, the maximum spacing the code table allows, the tighter layout spacing actually needed, a staggered placement table for every fastener, and a side-by-side comparison of all three fastener options.

Why This Matters

The ledger is the one connection that ties a deck to your house, and it is the connection most often blamed when a deck pulls away from the wall. Nails will not do the job, and bolts spaced "however looks right" will not either. The International Residential Code publishes a table of maximum fastener spacing that depends on how far your deck joists span. A longer span puts more load on the ledger, so the fasteners must sit closer together.

Take a 16-ft-wide deck with joists spanning 12 ft. With ½″ lag screws the table allows 15″ on center, which works out to 14 lag screws. Switch to ½″ through-bolts and the same deck needs only 8. Homeowners pricing hardware, contractors preparing permit drawings and inspectors checking a layout all need that number quickly, and they need to know which way it rounds. This calculator always rounds toward more fasteners and tighter spacing, never fewer.

How It's Calculated

The tool follows Table R507.9.1.3(1) of the 2021 International Residential Code, "Deck ledger connection to band joist." Earlier editions number the table differently, and your jurisdiction may use another edition or local amendments. The table gives the maximum fastener spacing for three connection types across seven bands of deck joist span: 6 ft or less, then up to 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 and 18 ft. The spacing depends on joist span only. Joist spacing and deck width do not enter the table. Spans are rounded up to the next band, so 12.1 ft is treated as 14 ft.

  • ½″ lag screw: 30, 23, 18, 15, 13, 11, 10 inches
  • ½″ through-bolt: 36, 36, 34, 29, 24, 21, 19 inches
  • ½″ through-bolt with ½″ stacked washers: 36, 36, 29, 24, 21, 18, 16 inches

The layout then runs in five steps:

  1. Max spacing = table value for your fastener and span band.
  2. Usable length = ledger length × 12 − 2 × 3″ end distance.
  3. Spaces = ⌈usable length ÷ max spacing⌉ (rounded up).
  4. Fasteners = spaces + 1.
  5. Layout spacing = usable length ÷ spaces, always at or below the table maximum.

Fasteners alternate between an upper row and a lower row, because the code requires them to be staggered. Spacing is measured along the ledger between neighboring fasteners, which is a conservative reading. The 3″ end distance is above the 2″ minimum. Spans over 18 ft fall outside the table, so the tool gives no answer for them.

Worked Example

A 16-ft ledger on a deck with 12-ft joists, using ½″ lag screws on a 2×10 ledger:

  1. Ledger length = 16 × 12 = 192″. Usable length = 192 − 6 = 186″.
  2. A 12-ft span falls in the "over 10 to 12 ft" band. For lag screws the maximum spacing is 15″.
  3. Spaces = ⌈186 ÷ 15⌉ = ⌈12.4⌉ = 13. Fasteners = 14.
  4. Layout spacing = 186 ÷ 13 = 14.31″, under the 15″ limit. Seven lags go in the upper row and seven in the lower row.

The first lag sits 3″ from the left end, the second at 17.31″, and the last at 189″. With through-bolts (29″ limit) the same deck needs 8 bolts at 26.57″. With stacked washers (24″ limit) it needs 9 at 23.25″.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure the joist span correctly. It runs from the ledger to the beam, not the width of the deck. If the joists cantilever past the beam, use the full joist length to be safe.
  • Stagger the fasteners. A straight line of bolts can split the ledger along the grain. Alternate upper and lower rows as in the diagram.
  • Do not substitute fasteners. Nails and ordinary deck screws are not listed in the table. Listed structural screws follow their own evaluation report, not this table.
  • Check what is behind the ledger. The table assumes a solid-sawn or engineered band joist. Do not attach to brick veneer, hollow cavities, or a cantilevered floor section, and flash the ledger properly.
  • Do not forget lateral load connectors. The code requires hold-down tension devices in addition to ledger fasteners, so ask your building department about them.
  • Never widen a spacing to dodge an obstacle. If a joist hanger or wall stud is in the way, shift the fastener slightly or add another one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size lag bolts do I need for a deck ledger?

The IRC table covers ½″ diameter lag screws and through-bolts. Choose a length that passes through the ledger and any sheathing and fully engages the band joist, commonly 3½″ to 4″ for lags. Through-bolts usually run 5″ to 6″ once washers and the nut are included.

How far apart should lag bolts be on a deck ledger?

For ½″ lag screws the maximum is 30″ for spans up to 6 ft, down to 10″ for 16 to 18 ft spans. A 12-ft span allows 15″. Through-bolts can be spaced farther apart than lags.

Are through-bolts better than lag screws for a ledger?

The table allows wider spacing for through-bolts, so you need fewer of them. They cost more to install because you need access to the inside of the band joist. Lag screws are easier to install from the outside but need more fasteners.

Can I use structural screws like LedgerLOK instead?

Many jurisdictions accept listed structural screws with a current evaluation report. Their spacing comes from the manufacturer's tables, not from this calculator, so follow those instructions and confirm with your inspector.

What if my deck joists span more than 18 feet?

The code table stops at 18 ft, so the tool gives no result. Longer spans call for an engineered design or a freestanding deck with its own support. Ask your building department or a structural engineer.

Do I need a permit for a deck attached with a ledger?

In most jurisdictions yes. Attached decks usually require a permit and a framing inspection. The inspector will check the fasteners, flashing and lateral connectors against the adopted code.
