Why This Matters

Steel is sold, shipped and lifted by weight, so getting the number right early saves money and prevents hazards. A fabricator quoting a job needs the weight of every bar and plate to price material. A trucker needs to know whether a bundle stays under the payload limit. A rigger needs to know whether a plate is a two-person carry or a crane lift. A single 1,200 × 2,400 × 10 mm plate weighs about 226 kg (498 lb), far beyond what two people should handle by hand. A bundle of fifty 25 mm round bars, each 6 metres long, weighs roughly 1,155 kg before you add the strapping and dunnage.

Homeowners and builders use the same maths when planning a steel beam delivery, a garage lintel, a gate frame or a workshop mezzanine. Estimators use it to compare carbon steel with stainless, where the extra density and the much higher price per kilogram make a real difference. Scrap yards and recyclers use theoretical weights to sanity-check scale tickets. In every case, a quick and consistent calculation beats guessing, and rounding up is safer than rounding down.

How It's Calculated

This calculator uses the standard theoretical-weight rule of thumb used by mills and suppliers: weight = cross-section area × length × density. Carbon steel is taken as 7,850 kg/m³, which is 0.2836 lb/in³ or about 490 lb/ft³. The result is a nominal, theoretical weight. It is not a certified mill weight under ASTM A6 or EN 10029, and tolerance rules and local codes vary.

Cross-section areas used:

  • Plate / flat bar: A = width × thickness
  • Round bar: A = π/4 × d² (in millimetres this gives the familiar d²/162 kg per metre)
  • Square bar: A = a²
  • Hex bar: A = (√3/2) × across-flats²
  • Round tube / pipe: A = π × t × (OD − t)
  • Rectangular tube: A = W×H − (W−2t)(H−2t)
  • Angle: A = t × (A + B − t)

Tube corner radii and angle root fillets are ignored, which changes weight by only a small percentage. Weight per metre is A × density. The piece weight multiplies by length, and the total multiplies by quantity. The planning total adds your allowance percentage and is rounded up, never down, so the figure errs on the heavy side. Imperial results convert using 1 kg = 2.2046 lb and 1 m = 3.2808 ft. A useful check for plate: steel weighs 7.85 kg per square metre for every millimetre of thickness, or 40.8 lb per square foot for every inch.

Worked Example

Take one carbon steel plate, 1,200 mm wide, 10 mm thick and 2.4 m long, with the default 5% allowance.

  1. Area: 1.2 m × 0.010 m = 0.012 m², which is 12,000 mm² or 18.60 in².
  2. Volume: 0.012 m² × 2.4 m = 0.02880 m³.
  3. Weight per metre: 0.012 × 7,850 = 94.200 kg/m, which is 63.299 lb/ft.
  4. Weight of the piece: 94.2 × 2.4 = 226.08 kg, or 498.42 lb.
  5. Add 5%: 226.08 × 1.05 = 237.384 kg, rounded up to 237.39 kg, which is 523.35 lb.

If steel costs 1.10 per kg, the estimated material cost is 237.39 × 1.10 = 261.13 (rounded up). Use the 237.39 kg figure when planning handling and transport, and the 226.08 kg figure as the nominal weight.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Check your units. Entering inches while the tool is set to millimetres makes the answer 645 times too small. Look at the unit in each label before you calculate.
  • Pipe size is not outside diameter. A 2-inch nominal pipe has an outside diameter of 2.375 in. Always enter the actual OD and wall thickness from the supplier's data sheet.
  • Keep the allowance. Mill tolerances, galvanizing (often several percent), welds and attachments add weight that nominal dimensions miss. Do not set it to zero for lifting or transport planning.
  • Add cut-off waste to your order. The tool weighs the pieces you enter. Offcuts, kerf and stock-length rounding are extra when you buy material.
  • Do not use this for lifting approval. Rigging, crane capacity and structural decisions need certified weights, rated equipment and a qualified person.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the weight of a steel plate?

Multiply width × thickness × length to get volume, then multiply by 7,850 kg/m³. For quick estimates, use 7.85 kg per square metre per millimetre of thickness, or 40.8 lb per square foot per inch of thickness.

What is the density of steel?

Carbon steel is normally taken as 7,850 kg/m³ (0.2836 lb/in³). Stainless grades are a little heavier, about 7,700 to 8,000 kg/m³ depending on the alloy, and gray cast iron is lighter at roughly 7,200 kg/m³.

What is the weight formula for round steel bar?

In millimetres, weight per metre in kilograms is about diameter² ÷ 162. A 25 mm bar is 625 ÷ 162, or roughly 3.85 kg per metre. This calculator uses the exact π/4 × d² area and your chosen density.

Why does the result include an allowance?

Real steel varies from nominal size, and coatings, welds and fittings add weight. The allowance gives you a safer planning number, and the final figure is rounded up rather than down.

Can I use this steel weight calculator for crane lifts?

No, treat it as an estimate only. Lifting plans must use certified weights, include the weight of rigging and attachments, and be approved by a qualified rigger or engineer under the rules that apply where you work.

Does the tool work for stainless steel and cast iron?

Yes. Pick stainless 304, 316 or 430 or gray cast iron from the material list, or enter a custom density in kg/m³ for any other alloy. The comparison table shows how the same part weighs in each material.
