How to Use This Concrete Weight Calculator

Pick a shape: rectangular for slabs, footings, walls and blocks, or round for Sonotube columns and piers. Type each dimension with its own unit, so you can enter a slab as 20 ft by 12 ft by 4 in without converting anything. Then choose a concrete type (or a custom density), set how much allowance you want, and press Calculate. You get the volume, the planning weight in pounds, kilograms and tons, and a table showing how the same volume weighs with other concrete types.

Why This Matters

Concrete is far heavier than it looks. One cubic yard weighs roughly 4,000 pounds, about as much as a mid-size SUV. A modest 10 × 10 ft patio at 4 inches thick weighs around 5,000 pounds, which is two and a half short tons. People need the number for more reasons than curiosity.

Homeowners use it to check whether a pickup, trailer or wheelbarrow route can handle a load. A half-ton truck often has a payload near 1,500 pounds, so a single yard of concrete can overload it. Deck builders and renovators use it to estimate dead load when a slab, a planter or a hearth pad will sit on a framed floor, balcony or roof. Contractors use it to plan cranes, forklifts and skid-steers, and to estimate haul-off weight when demolishing an old slab, where disposal is often billed by the ton. Precast makers and landscapers use it to size lifting hardware for steps, bollards, counterweights and pavers.

Getting it wrong in the light direction is the costly mistake, which is why this tool rounds up.

How It's Calculated

The calculator finds the volume of the shape, then multiplies by the density of the concrete.

Rectangular volume = length × width × thickness
Round volume = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × height
Weight = volume × density × pieces × (1 + allowance)

Every dimension is converted to feet first, so volume is in cubic feet. Cubic yards are cubic feet ÷ 27, and cubic metres are cubic feet × 0.0283168. Density is taken in pounds per cubic foot. Kilograms use the exact factor 0.45359237, short tons are pounds ÷ 2,000, and metric tonnes are kilograms ÷ 1,000.

The densities are common rule-of-thumb values: about 145 lb/ft³ for plain normal-weight concrete, about 150 lb/ft³ once steel reinforcement is included, roughly 110 lb/ft³ for structural lightweight mixes, and about 200 lb/ft³ for heavyweight mixes made with barite or magnetite aggregate. Real mixes vary with aggregate type, air content and moisture, typically by a few percent. Design codes and engineers may specify different unit weights, and local codes vary.

For safety, the planning weight includes your allowance and is then rounded up: pounds to the next 10, kilograms to the next 5, and tons to the next 0.01. This is a planning estimate, not engineering, permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

You are pouring a 10 ft × 10 ft slab, 4 inches thick, with reinforcing steel, and you use a 5% allowance.

  • Convert thickness: 4 in ÷ 12 = 0.3333 ft.
  • Volume: 10 × 10 × 0.3333 = 33.33 ft³, which is 1.23 yd³ or 0.94 m³.
  • Net weight at 150 lb/ft³: 33.33 × 150 = 5,000 lb.
  • Add 5%: 5,000 × 1.05 = 5,250 lb, which is 2,381 kg before rounding up to 2,385 kg.
  • In tons: 2.63 short tons, or 2.39 metric tonnes (both rounded up).

That is exactly what the calculator shows with its default inputs. Hauling this in a pickup with a 1,500 lb payload would take four trips (5,250 ÷ 1,500, rounded up), which is why most people have it delivered by ready-mix truck.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up inches and feet. A 4-inch slab entered as 4 feet is 12 times too heavy. Check the unit dropdown beside each number.
  • Forgetting thickened edges. Slabs with thickened perimeter footings contain more concrete than a flat plate. Calculate the footing as a separate piece and add the weights.
  • Using the net number for equipment. Use the weight with allowance, and make sure lifting gear, trailers and ramps are rated well above it.
  • Ignoring the supporting structure. If concrete will rest on a deck, balcony, roof or suspended floor, have a structural engineer confirm the framing can carry it. This tool does not do that.
  • Assuming every mix is 150 lb/ft³. Lightweight and heavyweight mixes differ by a large margin. If you know your mix design density, use the custom option.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a cubic yard of concrete weigh?

Plain normal-weight concrete weighs about 3,900 pounds per cubic yard (145 lb/ft³ × 27), and reinforced concrete about 4,050 pounds. Use roughly 4,000 lb, or 1.8 metric tonnes, as a quick estimate.

How much does concrete weigh per cubic foot?

Typically 145 to 150 pounds per cubic foot, or about 2,320 to 2,400 kg per cubic metre. Lightweight mixes can be as low as 90 to 115 lb/ft³.

How much does a 4-inch concrete slab weigh per square foot?

A 4-inch slab is one third of a cubic foot per square foot of area, so it weighs about 48 to 50 pounds per square foot. A 6-inch slab weighs about 72 to 75 lb/ft².

Does wet concrete weigh more than cured concrete?

Freshly mixed concrete is slightly heavier than fully cured concrete because some mixing water evaporates or is chemically bound as it cures. The difference is usually a few percent, and this calculator's allowance covers it. When moving a load of fresh concrete, plan for the higher number.

Can my pickup truck carry a yard of concrete?

Usually not. A cubic yard weighs about 4,000 pounds, which exceeds the payload of most half-ton pickups (often 1,000 to 1,800 lb). Check the payload sticker on your door jamb and never exceed it.

Does rebar change the weight of concrete?

Yes, slightly. Steel is about 490 lb/ft³, so reinforcement raises the average density by roughly 3 to 5 lb/ft³ at typical rebar ratios. That is why the reinforced option uses 150 lb/ft³ instead of 145.
