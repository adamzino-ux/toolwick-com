How to Use This Flooring Underlayment Calculator

Pick feet or meters, choose the underlayment product you plan to buy, then enter the room's length and width. Add any closets or hallways in the "additional area" box, slide the waste allowance to suit the room's shape, and press Calculate. If your product is not listed, choose "Custom coverage" and type the square footage printed on the package.

The result shows how many rolls or sheets to buy, the total coverage you will own, seam tape for roll products, a fastener count for plywood and cement board, and an optional cost. A comparison table shows the same room across every product type, and the diagram above redraws to show how strips or panels lay across your floor.

Why This Matters

Underlayment is cheap compared with the floor on top of it, which makes running short an expensive mistake. A 15 × 12 ft bedroom needs 180 square feet of coverage. Buy a single 100 sq ft roll by accident and you are driving back to the store mid-installation, hoping the next roll comes from the same batch. Over-buying is almost as irritating, because opened rolls of foam and cork are rarely returnable.

Different products also pack differently. A 4×8 plywood sheet covers 32 sq ft, but awkward rooms force cuts that leave unusable offcuts, so sheet goods need more waste than rolls. Cement board comes in 3×5 sheets of 15 sq ft, so even a small bathroom of 45 sq ft means several sheets plus screws. Roll length matters too: a long hallway or a 40 ft great room cannot always be covered from one continuous strip, and leftover pieces are not always long enough to reuse. Contractors, DIY renovators, landlords flipping units and anyone pricing a laminate, vinyl plank or engineered wood job benefit from getting the underlayment count right before ordering.

The right underlayment also affects warranties, sound control in apartments, and moisture protection over concrete. This tool handles quantity only. Always confirm the product type and thickness your flooring manufacturer requires, and check any building or HOA rules for sound ratings before buying.

How It's Calculated

The calculator works in square feet internally and converts meters using 1 m² = 10.7639 sq ft. Waste is applied to the total floor area, and the result is divided by the coverage per unit and always rounded up.

Floor area = length × width + additional area
Area to cover = floor area × (1 + waste %)
Units by area = ROUNDUP(area to cover ÷ coverage per unit)

For roll products there is a second, layout-based check. Strips run along the room's longer side. Strips = ROUNDUP(shorter side ÷ roll width). If the longer side fits within one roll length, the calculator counts how many full-length strips fit per roll (rounding down) and divides. If the room is longer than a roll, strips are joined, so total strip length is divided by the roll length. Additional area is added as extra linear footage. The final recommendation is whichever is larger, the area-based count or the layout-based count. This matters in long, narrow rooms, where offcuts are too short to reuse.

Seam tape = seams × longer side, scaled up for any additional area, padded 10% and rounded up to 100 ft tape rolls. For plywood and cement board, fasteners follow a common rule of thumb of an 8-inch grid, about 2.25 per sq ft of the area to cover, rounded up to the next 50. Roll sizes are typical retail sizes and vary by brand. Panel manufacturers publish their own fastening schedules, and those always override this estimate. The layout check assumes rolls are butted edge to edge, not overlapped, and that strips run parallel to the long wall. Doorways, stair openings and odd-shaped rooms may need more.

Worked Example

A bedroom is 15 ft × 12 ft with no closets, a 10% waste allowance, foam rolls of 100 sq ft (3.33 ft wide, 30 ft long), and a price of $48 per roll.

  • Floor area: 15 × 12 = 180.00 sq ft
  • Area to cover with waste: 180 × 1.10 = 198.00 sq ft
  • Rolls by area: 198 ÷ 100 = 1.98, rounded up to 2
  • Strips: 12 ÷ 3.33 = 3.6, so 4 strips of 15 ft each, giving 3 seams. A 30 ft roll yields 2 strips, so 4 strips need 2 rolls. The layout check agrees: 2 rolls (200 sq ft purchased, 20 sq ft over the floor area)
  • Seam tape: 3 × 15 = 45 ft, plus 10% = 49.5 ft, so 1 tape roll
  • Cost: 2 × $48 = $96.00 before tax and tape

Switch the product to 4×8 plywood and the same room needs 198 ÷ 32 = 6.19, rounded up to 7 sheets, and about 450 fasteners (198 × 2.25 = 445.5, rounded up to the next 50).

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Check what your flooring requires. Some floating floors have attached padding, and adding a second layer can void the warranty or cause joint failure.
  • Don't forget closets and hallways. They are the most common reason people come up short. Add them in the additional area field.
  • Use more waste for sheet goods. Plywood and cement board leave offcuts; 15% is a safer start than 10%.
  • Watch roll length in long rooms. If your room is longer than a roll, plan where the butt joints fall and stagger them from row to row.
  • Test for moisture on concrete. Foam underlayment without an attached vapor barrier will not stop moisture; follow the flooring maker's instructions.
  • Never overlap rolls unless told to. Most foam underlayment is butted and taped. Overlaps create ridges that telegraph through the floor.
  • Always verify package coverage and dimensions. Sizes vary by brand. Use Custom coverage if yours differs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much underlayment do I need for a 200 sq ft room?

With a 10% waste allowance you need 220 sq ft of coverage. That means three 100 sq ft rolls (300 sq ft) or two 200 sq ft rolls (400 sq ft). The calculator rounds up so you do not run short, and the comparison table shows the surplus for each option.

Do I need underlayment under laminate or vinyl plank?

Usually yes for laminate, unless it has a pad attached. Many vinyl plank products have a built-in backing and some manufacturers forbid extra underlayment. Check your installation guide first.

How much waste should I add to underlayment?

Ten percent works for square or rectangular rooms covered with rolls. Use 15% or more for sheet goods, diagonal layouts or rooms with many corners and doorways.

Is seam tape always needed?

Most foam and cork roll products are butted edge to edge and sealed with tape, which prevents shifting and moisture or adhesive creeping through. Plywood and cement board use fasteners instead, and some products have self-adhesive strips built in.

Can I use this calculator for self-leveling or roof underlayment?

No. Those are different products with different coverage math, such as depth and bag yield or roof squares. Use the dedicated calculators linked below.

Does this tool replace manufacturer or code requirements?

No. It estimates quantities only. Product type, thickness, fastener schedules and sound or moisture requirements come from the manufacturer and local building rules, which vary. It is not an inspection or permit approval.
