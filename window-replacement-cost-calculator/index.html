Why This Matters

Windows are one of the largest single line items in a home exterior project, and quotes can differ by thousands of dollars for what sounds like the same job. A vinyl double-hung insert window of about 36 × 60 inches typically runs $450 to $900 installed, while a full-frame wood replacement of the same size commonly lands between $1,100 and $1,600. Replacing ten windows therefore swings between roughly $5,000 and $16,000 depending on the choices you make.

Knowing the numbers before contractors visit lets you spot quotes that are far too high or suspiciously low. It also helps you decide whether to replace everything at once or phase the work, which windows deserve triple-pane glass (north-facing bedrooms in cold climates, for example) and how much contingency to hold back if the sheathing behind old trim turns out to be rotten. Homeowners preparing to sell use an estimate like this to judge return on investment, and landlords use it to budget a building-wide replacement across several units. If you are also planning other exterior work, the same discipline of pricing it out first applies to a roof or a basement, which is why those calculators are linked below.

How It's Calculated

The calculator uses planning rates averaged from typical U.S. retail and installer pricing. Each window's product cost is priced by area, then adjusted for frame and glass. Labor depends on install type, window size and style. A regional factor then scales the labor-type items.

area = width × height ÷ 144 (sq ft)
product = max($120, area × style rate × frame × glass)
labor = install base × style factor × size factor × region
size factor = area ÷ 15, limited to 0.7–2.0
total = (product + labor + extras) × (1 + contingency)

Style rates per square foot of vinyl, Low-E double-pane unit are: double-hung $28, casement $36, slider $26, picture $22, awning $34, and bay or bow $75. Frame multipliers are aluminum 0.90, vinyl 1.00, fiberglass 1.35, composite 1.50 and wood 1.65. Glass multipliers are clear double-pane 0.90, Low-E 1.00 and triple-pane 1.25. Base labor is $175 per window for an insert and $375 for full-frame. It is multiplied by a style factor (picture 0.8, double-hung and slider 1.0, casement and awning 1.1, bay or bow 2.5) and by the size factor above.

The regional setting multiplies labor, removal, trim, lead-safe work and permit fees by 0.85 for a lower-cost area, 1.00 for average and 1.25 for a high-cost metro. It does not change the product price or grids. The extras below are stated at the average (1.00) setting: removal and disposal $30 per window, trim $4.50 per linear foot of window perimeter, lead-safe work $75 per window, and permits $100 plus $10 per window. Decorative grids are a flat $35 per window.

The total is rounded up to the next $10 so the estimate never lands on the cheap side. The range shows 15% below to 20% above that total. Code notes in the results follow the 2021 IRC (R310 egress, R308 safety glazing) and 2021 IECC fenestration requirements, but local codes vary.

Worked Example

Say you replace 8 vinyl double-hung windows, each 36 × 60 inches, with Low-E double-pane glass as insert replacements in an average-cost area. You include removal and disposal and a 10% contingency, with no grids, trim, permit or lead-safe work.

  • Area per window: 36 × 60 ÷ 144 = 15 sq ft.
  • Product: 15 × $28 × 1.00 × 1.00 = $420 each, so 8 × $420 = $3,360.
  • Labor: $175 × 1.0 (style) × 1.0 (size) × 1.0 (region) = $175 each, so 8 × $175 = $1,400.
  • Removal and disposal: 8 × $30 = $240.
  • Subtotal: $3,360 + $1,400 + $240 = $5,000.
  • Contingency: 10% of $5,000 = $500, giving a total of $5,500.

That works out to about $688 per window and $45.83 per square foot of window, with a typical range of $4,670 to $6,600. These are the exact numbers the calculator shows with its default inputs.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Measure the existing opening, not the old sash. Measure width and height at three points and use the smallest. For an exact opening check, use the rough opening calculator.
  • Get three itemized quotes. Compare product line, glass package, U-factor and what is included for disposal, trim and permits, not just the bottom line.
  • Don't skip the contingency. Older homes often hide rotted sills or missing flashing. Ten percent is sensible, and 15–20% is safer for houses over 50 years old.
  • Check egress and safety glazing. Bedroom windows generally need a clear opening that meets egress rules, and glass near doors, tubs and stairs usually must be tempered. Both can change the price.
  • Don't pay for features you won't notice. Triple-pane and wood frames pay off in some climates and rooms, but not every window needs them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace one window?

A single standard vinyl double-hung window typically costs $450 to $900 installed. Wood, fiberglass, large or specialty units can run $1,000 to $2,500 or more. Use the calculator with a count of 1 to see your own number.

How much does it cost to replace all windows in a house?

A typical 2,000 sq ft home has 12 to 20 windows, which usually lands between $8,000 and $20,000 for mid-grade vinyl or fiberglass. Whole-house price depends heavily on window count, style and local labor rates.

What is the difference between insert and full-frame replacement?

An insert window fits inside the existing frame and leaves interior and exterior trim in place, so it costs less. Full-frame replacement removes everything down to the rough opening, which lets you fix rot and flashing. In this calculator it adds roughly $200 to $500 per window in labor for common sizes, more for large or bay units.

Are triple-pane windows worth the extra cost?

Triple-pane glass adds roughly 25% to the product cost in this calculator. It helps most in very cold climates, on noisy streets and for large north-facing windows. In mild climates, Low-E double-pane is usually the better value.

Do I need a permit to replace windows?

Many jurisdictions require a permit if you change the opening size or structure, and some require one even for like-for-like swaps. Rules vary by city and county, so call your local building department before work begins.

Why does my contractor quote differ from this estimate?

This calculator uses national-average planning rates. Brand, product line, wall type, access and local demand all move real quotes, so treat the estimate as a budgeting range and not a bid.
