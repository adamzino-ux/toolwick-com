How to Use This Drywall Installation Cost Calculator

Enter the room length, width and ceiling height, choose whether you are covering walls, the ceiling or both, then add your door and window counts. Pick a drywall type, finish level and who does the work. The calculator returns an estimated total with a likely range, a line-by-line breakdown, the number of sheets to buy, and a side-by-side comparison of all three finish levels.

Why This Matters

Drywall looks cheap until you add it all up. A standard 4×8 sheet costs about $15, but a 12×14 room with a ceiling needs roughly 19 sheets, plus screws, tape, several buckets of joint compound, and the labor to hang and finish it. Homeowners planning a basement finish, a garage conversion or a post-water-damage repair routinely underestimate by 30–50% because they price only the sheets.

A realistic estimate matters most when you are comparing bids. If one contractor quotes $1,100 and another $2,400 for the same room, you need to know whether the gap comes from the finish level (Level 5 skim coating can add about $0.50 per square foot over Level 4), ceiling work, or inflated materials. It also helps if you are weighing DIY against hiring out. Hanging board is physical but learnable, while taping and sanding is where beginners usually struggle. Landlords, flippers and small contractors can also use the estimate to build a materials order, set a deposit, or sanity-check a subcontractor price before adding markup.

How It's Calculated

All quantities are rounded up to whole units, which is the conservative direction: more sheets, more compound, never fewer. Rates are typical rules of thumb, not a quote, and local prices and codes vary.

Wall area = 2 × (L + W) × H − (doors × 21 + windows × 15)
Ceiling area = L × W
Net area = wall area + ceiling area
Sheets = ROUNDUP( net area × (1 + waste %) ÷ sheet area )
  • Screws: 1 lb per 100 sq ft, rounded up, at $4.00 per lb.
  • Joint tape: one 500 ft roll per 400 sq ft, rounded up, at $6.00 per roll.
  • Joint compound: 0.7, 1.0 or 1.6 gallons per 100 sq ft for Levels 3, 4 and 5, bought in 4.5-gallon buckets at $20.00.
  • Supplies allowance: 5% of those materials for corner bead, sandpaper and primer-level extras.
  • Labor: hanging at $0.75 per sq ft, plus finishing at $0.70 (Level 3), $0.95 (Level 4) or $1.45 (Level 5). Ceiling area counts at 1.25× because overhead work is slower. Both rates are multiplied by your regional slider.
  • Range: the total ±15%, reflecting normal bid variation.

Sheet counts follow common practice under ASTM C840 and GA-216 (horizontal hanging, staggered seams). The tool is an estimating aid only. It is not a bid, an engineering document, or permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A 12 ft × 14 ft room with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows, walls and ceiling, 1/2" regular board at $15 per 4×8 sheet, Level 4 finish, 10% waste, labor multiplier 1.0 (these are the tool's default values):

  1. Gross wall area: 2 × (12 + 14) × 8 = 416 sq ft. Openings: 21 + 2 × 15 = 51 sq ft. Net walls: 365 sq ft.
  2. Ceiling: 12 × 14 = 168 sq ft. Net area: 533 sq ft.
  3. With waste: 533 × 1.10 = 586.3 sq ft ÷ 32 = 18.3, rounded up to 19 sheets = $285.00.
  4. Screws: 6 lb = $24.00. Tape: 2 rolls = $12.00. Compound: 5.33 gal ÷ 4.5 = 2 buckets = $40.00.
  5. Supplies at 5%: $18.05. Materials total: $379.05.
  6. Labor area: 365 + 168 × 1.25 = 575 sq ft. Hanging: 575 × $0.75 = $431.25. Finishing: 575 × $0.95 = $546.25.
  7. Total: $379.05 + $431.25 + $546.25 = $1,356.55, about $2.55 per sq ft, with a likely range of $1,153 to $1,560.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Don't price sheets alone. Finishing materials and labor typically account for more than half of a professional job.
  • Check fire and moisture requirements first. Walls between a garage and living space, and ceilings under habitable rooms, usually call for fire-rated board (see IRC R302.6; local codes vary). Use moisture-resistant board near tubs and showers.
  • Use 5/8" on ceilings when framing is 24" on center. Thinner 1/2" board can sag over time, and 5/8" is the usual fix.
  • Raise the waste factor for small or chopped-up rooms. Closets, stairwells and angled walls produce more offcuts than open rectangles.
  • Wear a P100 or N95 mask when sanding. Joint-compound dust is fine and irritating, so plan for dust control and cleanup.
  • Get three quotes. Use this estimate as a baseline, then ask each contractor what finish level and ceiling work are included.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does drywall installation cost per square foot?

Professional installation commonly runs about $2 to $3.50 per square foot with materials, hanging and finishing included. Level 5 finishes, ceilings, small jobs and high-cost metro areas land at the top of that range, and this calculator defaults to roughly $2.50 for a standard room.

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12×14 room?

With 8 ft ceilings, one door, two windows and 10% waste, you need about 13 sheets of 4×8 for the walls alone, or 19 sheets if you also cover the ceiling. Using 4×12 sheets on the ceiling means fewer seams to finish.

Is it cheaper to hang drywall yourself?

Yes, DIY removes roughly 70% of the cost in this model because labor is the biggest line. The catch is finishing: taping and sanding take skill, and a poor Level 4 finish shows through paint, so many DIYers hang the board and hire out the finishing.

What is the difference between Level 3, 4 and 5 finishes?

Level 3 (two coats on joints) suits walls that will be textured. Level 4 (three coats) is the standard for flat paint, and Level 5 adds a full skim coat for glossy paint or strong side lighting, per the GA-214 guideline. Each step uses more compound and labor, so cost rises.

Should I subtract doors and windows from the area?

This tool does, using 21 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window. Many installers do not deduct small openings because cutting around them produces waste, so if you have lots of openings, raise the waste slider rather than ignoring them.

Does this estimate include painting, framing or demolition?

No. It covers drywall, fasteners, tape, compound, a small supplies allowance, and labor to hang and finish. Framing, insulation, demolition, primer, paint, texture spray, permits and electrical work are separate costs.
