How to Use This Drywall Texture Calculator

Choose whether to enter room dimensions or a known total area. For dimensions, type the length, width and ceiling height in feet, pick whether you are texturing walls, the ceiling or both, and enter the number of doors and windows so their area is subtracted. Then select a texture style, product form (powder or premixed), number of coats and a waste allowance. The result shows how many bags or buckets to buy, rounded up, along with a comparison of every texture style for the same area. If your product label states a coverage figure, type it into the optional label field and the calculator will use it instead of the built-in planning rate.

Why This Matters

Texture compound is one of those materials where running short costs far more than buying extra. Spray texture has to be applied wet and in one continuous session. If you run out halfway across a ceiling, the lap line where you stop and restart often shows after paint, because new batches rarely match the old one in thickness or drying time. Having the right amount on site before you start avoids a mid-job store run and a visible seam.

The opposite problem is just as real. A homeowner retexturing a 14 × 12 ft bedroom might guess "a few bags" and come home with five when two would do. Powder bags are cheap, but premixed buckets can run $20 to $35 each, and leftover mixed material rarely keeps well. Contractors bidding repair or remodel work also need a defensible material line item: a 1,800 sq ft house with 8 ft ceilings has roughly 4,000 sq ft of paintable wall and ceiling surface, and the difference between orange peel and a heavy skip trowel can change the material count by 40 percent or more.

Patch work is another common use. After a water-damage repair or a wall removal, you may only need to blend texture over 60 to 150 sq ft. Knowing roughly how far one bag stretches helps you buy a single small quantity instead of guessing.

How It's Calculated

The calculator first finds the surface area, subtracts openings, applies your coats and waste allowance, and divides by the coverage rate of the chosen product. It always rounds up to the next whole bag or bucket so you never end up short.

Walls = 2 × (L + W) × H
Ceiling = L × W
Net area = Walls + Ceiling − (doors × 21) − (windows × 15)
Area to cover = Net area × coats × (1 + waste %)
Units needed = ROUNDUP(Area to cover ÷ coverage per unit)

Door and window deductions are applied to walls only and never reduce the ceiling. The planning coverage rates used, in sq ft per unit for a single coat, are below.

For the four textured styles they are typical rule-of-thumb figures for powder, and the premixed bucket rate is assumed to be about 90 percent of the powder rate. That 90 percent is a planning assumption with no published basis, so treat it as approximate. The skim coat figures come from volume: a 4.5 gal bucket holds about 0.60 cu ft, and a 1/16 in layer is about 0.0052 ft thick, so one bucket spreads over roughly 115 sq ft (0.60 ÷ 0.0052). For powder, the calculator assumes about 4 gal of mixed compound per 50 lb bag, which is about 0.53 cu ft and roughly 100 sq ft at the same thickness. A thicker skim coat covers proportionally less. Always compare against the coverage printed on your product, and enter it in the label field for a more accurate count.

Worked Example

A bedroom is 14 ft long, 12 ft wide, with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows. You plan one coat of knockdown with 50 lb powder bags and a 10% waste allowance.

  • Walls: 2 × (14 + 12) × 8 = 416 sq ft
  • Ceiling: 14 × 12 = 168 sq ft, so gross area is 584 sq ft
  • Openings: 1 door × 21 + 2 windows × 15 = 51 sq ft, so net area is 533 sq ft
  • With 10% waste and 1 coat: 533 × 1.10 = 586.3 sq ft to cover
  • Knockdown powder is assumed to cover 400 sq ft per bag: 586.3 ÷ 400 = 1.47 bags, which rounds up to 2 bags
  • With premixed at 360 sq ft per bucket: 586.3 ÷ 360 = 1.63, so 2 buckets

At $22 per bag, the material cost is about $44.00.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Test for asbestos first. Textured ceilings installed before the 1980s can contain asbestos. Do not scrape or sand until a lab confirms it is safe, and follow local rules for removal.
  • Check the label. Coverage varies a lot between brands and mixing ratios. If the bag or bucket states a coverage, use the optional label field instead of the built-in rate.
  • Prime new drywall. Unprimed paper and joint compound absorb moisture unevenly, causing blotchy texture and varying coverage. Use a drywall primer first.
  • Practice on cardboard. Spray pattern depends on compressor pressure, nozzle size and mix thickness. Test on scrap before committing to the ceiling.
  • Mask everything. Overspray travels. Cover floors, windows, trim and fixtures, and wear a dust mask or respirator when mixing powder.
  • Buy all product at once. Different batches can vary slightly in color and consistency. Round up rather than down, and keep the extra for future patches.
  • Do not stretch coverage. Thinning compound too far to cover more area gives weak, patchy texture that can flake or fail to hold its profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much area does a bag of drywall texture cover?

It depends heavily on the style. This calculator assumes roughly 500 sq ft per 50 lb bag for light orange peel, about 400 for knockdown, down to about 100 sq ft for a 1/16 in smooth skim coat. These are rule-of-thumb assumptions, so check the coverage printed on your specific product.

How many bags of texture do I need for a 12×12 room?

A 12×12 room with 8 ft ceilings has about 384 sq ft of wall and 144 sq ft of ceiling, before deducting openings. With knockdown texture and a 10% waste allowance, that works out to roughly 2 bags of powder. Enter your exact numbers above for a precise count.

Is it cheaper to use powder or premixed texture?

Powder is generally cheaper per square foot, but you must mix it and get the consistency right. Premixed buckets cost more but save time, give a consistent mix, and are good for small repairs. The calculator shows both quantities so you can compare prices.

Do I need a second coat of texture?

Most spray textures like orange peel and knockdown are applied in one coat. A second coat is used for heavier profiles, to even out a thin area, or to cover a patchy first pass. Selecting 2 coats doubles the material estimate.

Should I subtract windows and doors?

Yes, for walls. The calculator deducts 21 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window, which are standard sizes. If your openings are unusually large, such as sliding glass doors, enter a larger count or use the total area mode with your own measured number.

What waste allowance should I use?

Ten percent suits most interior jobs. Use 15 to 20 percent for spray application with lots of overspray, tall or vaulted ceilings, or if you are a beginner. Use 5 percent only for small, simple surfaces applied by roller or trowel.
