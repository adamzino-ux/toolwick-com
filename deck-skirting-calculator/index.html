How to Use This Deck Skirting Calculator

Enter your deck's length and width, tick the sides you plan to close in, and enter the height from the ground to the underside of the decking. Choose vertical boards, horizontal boards or lattice panels, set the board size or panel size and add an access opening if you want one. The results list the boards or panels to buy, 2x4 framing pieces, screws, the net skirt area, a ventilation check and an optional cost estimate.

Why This Matters

Skirting is the finishing layer that turns a raised platform into a deck that looks built-in. It also keeps raccoons, skunks and groundhogs from nesting underneath, stops wind-blown leaves and trash from collecting, and gives you a hidden space for mowers, planters and winter storage. Done badly, it traps moisture, rots the bottom edge and blocks the airflow that keeps joists dry.

Ordering is where most people go wrong. A 16 × 12 ft deck sitting 36 inches off the ground, skirted on three sides, needs roughly 105 square feet of cladding. That is about 44 eight-foot boards, and short one box of boards from a different lumber lot and the color will not match. Running out of 2x4 studs mid-project means another trip with the truck. Deck skirting is also one of the first places builders cut corners on clearance. Wood that touches soil wicks moisture and fails years early, so a deliberate 2-inch gap matters. Use the calculator before you shop so you buy once, cut once and leave the ventilation you need.

How It's Calculated

The calculator measures the run of each skirted side, subtracts any access opening, then sizes the cladding and framing from the skirt height. Everything is rounded up, never down.

Skirt height = deck height − ground clearance
Net run = sum of skirted sides − opening width
Vertical boards per run = ⌈ run (in) ÷ (board width + gap) ⌉
Horizontal rows = ⌈ (skirt height + gap) ÷ (board width + gap) ⌉
Lattice panels = ⌈ run ÷ panel width ⌉ × ⌈ skirt height ÷ panel height ⌉
Studs per run = ⌈ run (in) ÷ spacing ⌉ + 1
Boards to buy = ⌈ cut pieces × (1 + waste) ÷ pieces per stock board ⌉

Any side with an opening is split into two runs, one on each side of the door, so the framing and cladding are counted separately for each. Rails are 2x4s running along the top and bottom (plus a middle rail when the skirt is taller than 48 inches). Screws are counted at two per board at each rail or stud crossing. Lattice screws are counted every 8 inches around each panel's perimeter.

The ventilation check compares the open area of your skirt with a rule of thumb of 1 square foot of open area per 150 square feet of deck. This borrows from the crawl-space ventilation language in IRC R408.1. Decks are not crawl spaces, and local codes vary. Lattice is assumed to be 40% open, which is conservative. For wood near the ground, the calculator assumes pressure-treated, ground-contact-rated lumber as described in IRC R317.1.

Worked Example

A 16 × 12 ft deck sits 36 inches above grade. You skirt the front, left and right sides, leave a 3 ft access door in the front, use 5.5 in vertical boards with a 0.25 in gap, buy 8-ft boards, space studs at 16 in and allow 10% waste.

  • Skirt height: 36 − 2 = 34 in.
  • Runs: the front becomes two 6.5 ft runs, plus 12 ft on each side, so the net run is 37 ft.
  • Boards: 14 + 14 + 26 + 26 = 80 boards, or 88 with waste.
  • Each 8-ft board yields 2 pieces of 34 in (96 ÷ 34 = 2.8, rounded down), so you buy 44 boards.
  • Framing: 6 + 6 + 10 + 10 = 32 studs, or 16 eight-foot 2x4s. Two rails over 37 ft is 74 ft, or 11 eight-foot pieces with waste.
  • Screws: 88 × 2 rails × 2 = 352 for the cladding and 128 for the framing.
  • Area: 37 × 34 ÷ 12 ≈ 104.8 sq ft. Open area is about 4.6 sq ft against a recommended 1.3 sq ft, so ventilation passes.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Keep 1–2 inches off the soil. Even treated lumber lasts longer when it can dry, and soil grades rise as mulch and leaves build up.
  • Do not seal the space airtight. Trapped humidity rots joists. Keep gaps between boards, or use lattice for at least some of the skirt.
  • Always include an access door. You will need to retrieve balls, reach plumbing or electrical lines and check for pests. Latch it so animals cannot push it open.
  • Buy all boards in one go. Color and moisture content vary between lots, especially with pressure-treated pine and composite.
  • Measure the actual grade, not the nominal height. Ground often slopes. Measure at several points, use the largest skirt height, and scribe or step the bottom edge to follow the slope.

In wildfire-prone regions, enclosing the underside may be restricted or require ember-resistant vents and noncombustible materials. Check with your local building department before you close it in.

Frequently Asked Questions

How high should deck skirting be off the ground?

Leave about 1 to 2 inches between the bottom of the skirt and the soil. This keeps wood from wicking ground moisture and allows for mulch or gravel to build up over time. The calculator defaults to 2 inches.

How much ventilation does the space under a deck need?

A common rule of thumb is 1 square foot of open area per 150 square feet of deck, borrowed from crawl-space guidance in IRC R408.1. Gaps between boards or lattice openings count toward that area. Your local code or climate may require more.

Should I use vertical or horizontal boards?

Vertical boards are quicker to install on 2x4 rails and shed water well. Horizontal boards look more like a fence or siding and need studs at 16 inches on center. Both work, and the calculator handles either.

How much waste should I allow?

Ten percent suits most straight runs. Go to 15% or more if you have many short runs, angled corners or a sloped grade, or if you want to pick out only the straightest boards.

Do I need a permit to add deck skirting?

Many areas do not require a permit for non-structural skirting on an existing deck, but some do, especially with enclosed framing or in wildfire zones. A quick call to your building department gives you a definite answer. This calculator is a materials estimate, not a code approval.

Should I skirt the side attached to the house?

No. The house wall already closes that side. Skirt only the exposed edges, and do not attach skirt framing to the ledger board or any structural part of the deck.
