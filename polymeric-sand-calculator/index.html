How to Use This Polymeric Sand Calculator

Start by entering the size of your paved area, either as length and width in feet or as a total square footage if the shape is irregular. Pick your paver size (or type a custom one), the paver thickness, the joint width, and the bag size you plan to buy. The waste slider covers sweeping loss, settling and uneven joints; 15% is a sensible default.

Press Calculate to see the number of bags to buy (always rounded up), the total pounds of sand, the coverage you can expect per bag, and a side-by-side table showing how other joint widths would change the order. Why This Matters

Polymeric sand is not cheap. A 50 lb bag usually costs $25 to $45, and a mid-size patio can take four to eight bags. Running short on the last row is a real problem, because the product has to be swept in, compacted, misted and left alone during a single dry window, usually 24 hours with no rain. A second trip to the store can also mean a different dye lot and a visible color mismatch in the joints.

Joint width changes the quantity more than most people expect. Going from 1/8 in to 3/8 in joints on 4 × 8 in pavers nearly triples the sand, because the joint share of the surface climbs from about 4.5% to about 12.7%. Paver size matters too: large 24 × 24 in slabs have far less joint length per square foot than small 4 × 4 in units, so they need much less sand.

Homeowners planning a weekend patio, landscapers quoting a walkway, and contractors ordering for a driveway all need the same thing: a defensible number before the truck leaves the yard. This tool gives you that number from the geometry of your joints instead of a one-size-fits-all coverage guess.

How It's Calculated

The calculator treats your paving as a repeating grid. Each paver plus one joint forms a cell, and the joint is whatever part of the cell is not paver.

cell area = (length + joint) × (width + joint)
joint share = 1 − (length × width) ÷ cell area
fill depth = paver thickness − 1/8 in
joint volume (cu ft) = area × joint share × fill depth ÷ 12
sand (lb) = joint volume × 100 lb/cu ft × (1 + waste %)
bags = sand ÷ bag size, rounded up to a whole bag

The assumptions are deliberately on the cautious side. The sand is assumed to fill the full joint depth, stopping 1/8 in below the paver surface, which is the usual manufacturer guidance so the sand does not sit proud and leave haze. Loose polymeric sand is taken at about 100 lb per cubic foot. The result is always rounded up to full bags, never down.

This is a rule-of-thumb estimate, not a code calculation, engineering design, or inspection approval. Product coverage charts differ between brands, chamfered or beveled paver edges add joint volume, and local rules or HOA requirements may apply. If your product label gives a coverage chart for your paver size, use the larger of the two answers.

Worked Example

A 15 ft × 12 ft patio of standard 4 × 8 in pavers, 2 3/8 in thick, with 1/8 in joints, 15% waste and 50 lb bags:

  1. Area: 15 × 12 = 180 sq ft.
  2. Cell area: (8 + 0.125) × (4 + 0.125) = 33.516 sq in. The paver itself is 32 sq in.
  3. Joint share: 1 − 32 ÷ 33.516 = 4.52% of the surface.
  4. Fill depth: 2.375 − 0.125 = 2.25 in = 0.1875 ft.
  5. Joint volume: 180 × 0.0452 × 0.1875 = 1.53 cu ft.
  6. Sand before waste: 1.53 × 100 = 152.6 lb.
  7. With 15% waste: 152.6 × 1.15 = 175.5 lb.
  8. Bags: 175.5 ÷ 50 = 3.51, rounded up to 4 bags, covering about 51 sq ft each.

Those are the same figures the calculator shows when you press the button with its default inputs.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Check the joint width limits on the bag. Many products are rated for 1/16 in to 1/2 in joints; wider joints need a product made for them or the sand may crack and wash out.
  • Don't skip the waste allowance. Sweeping, compaction and joints that settle after the first pass are why 10% to 20% extra is normal.
  • Buy all bags from the same lot. Color varies slightly between batches, so check the lot number on every bag and keep one unopened for later top-ups.
  • Keep the pavers dry and the forecast clear. Wet joints cause the sand to activate early and leave a hard haze on the surface. Plan for 24 hours of dry weather after activation.
  • Protect your lungs and eyes. Sand dust contains crystalline silica; wear an N95-type mask and eye protection when pouring and sweeping.
  • Make sure joints are deep enough. Most manufacturers want at least 1 in of joint depth; thin pavers or heavy bedding intrusion can fall short.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of polymeric sand do I need per 100 square feet?

It depends on paver size and joint width. For 4 × 8 in pavers, 2 3/8 in thick with 1/8 in joints, 100 sq ft needs about 97 lb including 15% waste, or two 50 lb bags. Larger pavers need less, and wider joints need more.

Does polymeric sand fill the whole joint?

Yes. Fill the joint to about 1/8 in below the paver surface, not just the top inch. Use regular jointing sand underneath only if the product label allows it. Full-depth fill gives the best hold and prevents the surface sand from sinking.

Can I use polymeric sand in 1/2 inch joints?

Some products are rated for joints up to 1/2 in or wider, but many standard formulas are limited to 1/4 or 3/8 in. Check the label. This calculator supports up to 1/2 in so you can plan quantities, but the right product is your call.

Should I buy extra polymeric sand?

Yes, at least one extra bag on a job over a few hundred square feet, or use the waste slider. Because the result is rounded up to full bags, you already carry a small buffer. Unopened bags can often be returned.

Do chamfered or beveled pavers need more sand?

Yes. Beveled edges create a V-shaped pocket at the top of each joint that this simple grid model does not count. Move the waste slider toward 20% to 25% for heavily beveled or tumbled pavers.

What is the difference between polymeric sand and regular joint sand?

Polymeric sand contains binders that harden when misted with water, which helps resist weeds, ants and washout. Regular joint sand stays loose and needs topping up. Polymeric sand costs more per pound, so getting the quantity right matters.
