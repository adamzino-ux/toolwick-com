How to Use This Bathroom Remodel Cost Calculator

Enter your bathroom's length, width and ceiling height in feet, then slide the finish level from budget to high-end. Choose your tub or shower, tile coverage, vanity and any plumbing changes, tick the extras you want, and press Calculate. You will see a total, a likely range, a cost per square foot, an itemized table and a three-way comparison of finish levels.

Why This Matters

A bathroom is the smallest room in most homes and often the most expensive per square foot to renovate. Plumbing, waterproofing, tile and electrical work are all packed into a few dozen square feet, and every trade has a minimum charge. That is why a 5×8 bathroom can cost $8,000 with basic finishes and $30,000 with custom tile and a frameless glass shower.

Homeowners use an estimate like this before calling contractors, so quotes make sense when they arrive. If one bid comes in at $9,000 and another at $19,000 for the "same" job, you need to know which line items explain the gap. It also helps when deciding between a full gut and a cosmetic refresh, or when setting aside funds from a home equity line. Landlords and flippers use it to check whether an upgrade will pay back before they commit. Because the calculator separates demolition, fixtures, tile and permits, you can see where trimming one choice, such as a shower surround or a layout change, frees up real money.

How It's Calculated

The calculator builds the budget from individual line items, each priced at a 2025 U.S. installed average (materials and labor) for the finish level you chose:

  • Area-based items: flooring, heated floor and demolition use floor area (length × width). Demolition adds a fixed haul-away charge.
  • Wall-based items: paint and drywall use wall area plus ceiling area. Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × ceiling height, minus 20 sq ft for a door and window. Full-height or half-height tile uses the same wall math.
  • Fixed items: toilet, vanity, tub or shower, fan, lighting, accessories and permit are priced per unit. A double vanity is 1.7× a single.

The formula is: Total = (sum of line items × location multiplier) + contingency. Location multipliers are 0.85, 1.00, 1.25 and 1.50. Contingency is your chosen percentage of the subtotal. The range shown is −15% to +25% of the total, because overruns are more common than savings.

Safety-minded rounding: every line item and the contingency are rounded up to the next dollar, and the range is rounded outward to the nearest $100. The tool prices work that model codes expect in a bathroom: GFCI protection (NEC 210.8), a 20-amp bathroom receptacle circuit (NEC 210.11(C)(3)), and ventilation by window or exhaust fan (IRC R303.3). Code editions and local amendments vary. This is a planning estimate only, not a bid, an engineering opinion, or permit or inspection approval.

Worked Example

A 5 × 8 ft bathroom with an 8 ft ceiling, mid-range finishes, national average pricing, and the default options above (tub/shower replacement, surround tile, single vanity, 15% contingency):

  1. Floor area = 8 × 5 = 40 sq ft. Wall area = 2 × (8 + 5) × 8 − 20 = 188 sq ft.
  2. Demolition: $900 + 40 × $14 = $1,460. Flooring: 40 × $22 = $880.
  3. Vanity $1,900, toilet $700, tub/shower $3,200, surround tile 60 × $22 = $1,320.
  4. Electrical $1,400, fan $500, paint (188 + 40) × $3.20 = $730 (rounded up), accessories $500, permit $500.
  5. Subtotal = $13,090. Contingency 15% = $1,964 (rounded up).
  6. Total = $15,054, about $377 per sq ft, with a range of $12,700 to $18,900.

Tips & Common Mistakes

  • Don't skip contingency. Opening walls often reveals rot, outdated wiring or failed waterproofing. In homes built before 1980, use 20–25%.
  • Keep plumbing where it is. Moving a toilet drain or shower valve can add $1,500–$4,500 before any finish is chosen.
  • Don't cut ventilation or electrical to save money. A missing exhaust fan leads to mold and failed paint, and GFCI protection is a basic safety requirement.
  • Test older homes first. Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint and older flooring or tile adhesive may contain asbestos. Testing and abatement are not in this estimate.
  • Order long-lead items early. Custom vanities and glass can take 6–10 weeks, and delays add labor and rental costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bathroom remodel cost on average?

A mid-range full bathroom remodel in the U.S. typically runs $12,000 to $20,000. Cosmetic refreshes start near $5,000, while high-end projects with custom tile and glass often pass $30,000.

How much does it cost to remodel a small 5×8 bathroom?

Most 40 sq ft bathrooms land between $9,000 and $18,000 depending on finishes and location. Small rooms cost more per square foot because fixtures and labor minimums don't shrink with the room.

What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?

The shower or tub area, including waterproofing, tile and glass, is usually the largest cost. Plumbing relocation and a double vanity are the next biggest budget swings.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

A standard remodel takes two to four weeks once materials are on site. Layout changes, custom tile or backordered fixtures can stretch it to six to ten weeks.

Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom?

Most jurisdictions require permits when you move plumbing, add circuits or change walls, but not for swapping a vanity or toilet. Rules vary, so call your local building department before starting.

How much contingency should I add?

Plan for 10–15% on newer homes and 20–25% on older ones or any job with layout changes. Hidden water damage or outdated wiring is the usual reason budgets get used up.
